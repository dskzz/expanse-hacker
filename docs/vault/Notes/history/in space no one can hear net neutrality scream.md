Exactly. That line is basically the unofficial SolNet motto.

What you’ve built is a network where **neutrality is a myth, not a goal**, and that’s not a flaw — it’s the only honest way to model a system stretched across power, distance, and scarcity.

In SolNet, packets don’t get treated equally because **nodes aren’t equal**. A UN relay sitting in Earth orbit with ledger access, regulatory backing, and surplus power is not going to behave like a Belter repeater running off scavenged solar and favors owed. And pretending they should would just push the real behavior underground.

That’s why the choices you just made line up so cleanly:

- **TLVs are abundant and namespaced** — everyone can speak, but not everyone gets listened to.
    
- **Parsing is neutral; interpretation is political** — the wire doesn’t care, the node absolutely does.
    
- **Ledger anchoring is a gradient** — authority hardens as you move inward, softens as you move outward, and eventually dissolves into “good enough for now.”
    

Net neutrality dies the moment:

- bandwidth costs energy,
    
- trust costs favors,
    
- and finality costs exposure.
    

SolNet doesn’t try to save it. It documents the corpse and builds guardrails around the smell.

The beautiful thing is that this gives you _mechanics_, not just lore:

- Earth routers preferring Earth namespaces isn’t bias — it’s policy.
    
- Mars ignoring OPA hints unless corroborated isn’t hostility — it’s risk management.
    
- Belter nodes skipping half the TLVs and trusting whoever paid last isn’t chaos — it’s survival.
    

And ledger anchoring being expensive, slow, or dangerous in the outer system turns “truth” into a strategic resource instead of a checkbox. Sometimes you don’t want to be right — you want to be _unprovable_ long enough to get away.

That’s a network that feels alive.

The next really sharp edge to decide is whether **policy preference itself is observable** — can nodes advertise “I prefer MCRN namespaces” or “I distrust unanchored receipts,” or is that something players have to infer by watching behavior over time?
