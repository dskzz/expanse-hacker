# Appendix D: Unauthorized Multi‑Path Broadcast Reconstruction

_(SSWG Internal — Circulation Restricted)_

This appendix documents the hardware and routing components recovered or inferred from the Ceres incident in which a former UNN officer constructed an unsanctioned, cross‑jurisdiction broadcast node capable of forcing high‑priority replication across Earth, Mars, and Belt relay domains. The incident demonstrates that the drift‑era network was vulnerable not only to silent failure but also to technically competent exploitation.

The following components were identified as necessary for the construction of the broadcast chain.

## 1. Timing and Synchronization Components

These items are not typically available on Ceres without mislabeling, diversion, or smuggling. Their presence indicates deliberate acquisition.

- **Class‑3 Fleet Timing Crystal Assemblies** — Required to maintain doctrinal timing windows for combat‑class metadata. Civilian timing drift would have demoted the message to low‑priority queues.
    
- **Redundant Phase‑Locked Loop Modules** — Used to maintain synchronization across multiple relay paths. Belt‑grade PLLs lack the stability needed for multi‑path replication.
    
- **Legacy Martian Tightbeam Sync Chips** — Not used for tightbeam transmission in this case, but for their superior timing stability. Their presence suggests access to Martian surplus or salvage.
    

_SSWG Note:_ “No civilian has a legitimate reason to own timing hardware with this level of stability. The only plausible explanation is intent to impersonate fleet‑class traffic.”

## 2. Transmission and Relay Hardware

The broadcast required simultaneous use of multiple propagation paths to overwhelm jurisdictional filtering.

- **Fleet‑Grade RF Transceivers (UNN Pattern 4B)** — Capable of high‑priority metadata tagging and multi‑path replication. These units are not sold commercially.
    
- **Martian Burst‑Mode Transmitters** — Used to seed the initial replication wave. Their burst characteristics make suppression difficult once propagation begins.
    
- **Belt Opportunistic Mesh Nodes** — Civilian‑grade, but modified to accept fleet metadata. These nodes provided the lateral spread that made suppression impossible.
    
- **Redundant Power Regulation Units** — Necessary to maintain stable output during burst transmission. Civilian regulators brown out under this load.
    

_SSWG Note:_ “The combination of UNN, MCRN, and Belt hardware is not accidental. It is the minimum viable set for a cross‑jurisdiction broadcast.”

## 3. Firmware and Metadata Components

The message succeeded because it was tagged in a way the drift‑era network treated as authoritative.

- **UNN Combat‑Class Metadata Firmware (v. 2.3.1)** — This version predates the Anderson suppression protocols and still triggers mandatory replication.
    
- **Martian Header Integrity Modules** — Used to generate checksums consistent with MIAP doctrine. This prevented Mars‑side relays from discarding the message.
    
- **Belt‑Modified Routing Tables** — These tables allowed the message to enter opportunistic mesh paths normally reserved for emergency telemetry.
    

_SSWG Note:_ “The operator clearly understood that metadata classification, not content, determines propagation priority.”

## 4. Physical Infrastructure

The broadcast node was assembled from components that would not attract attention individually but form a recognizable pattern when viewed together.

- **Three independent power buses** — Required for redundancy during burst replication.
    
- **Shielded cabling** — Prevents timing jitter under load.
    
- **Cooling assemblies** — Civilian units cannot dissipate the heat generated by fleet‑class burst transmission.
    
- **Antenna arrays with mixed‑jurisdiction tuning** — Capable of transmitting on Earth, Mars, and Belt emergency bands.
    

_SSWG Note:_ “This is not improvisation. This is reconstruction of a fleet‑class broadcast node using whatever parts were available.”

## 5. Why Dawes Noticed

From the perspective of a Ceres dockworker with political instincts and a long memory:

- UNN timing crystals do not belong in civilian cargo.
    
- Martian sync chips do not travel through Ceres without someone paying attention.
    
- Fleet‑grade RF transceivers do not “fall off a ship.”
    
- Cooling assemblies and power regulators of this class are not used in any Belt civilian application.
    
- The pattern of acquisition—small, distributed, deniable—indicates someone trying not to be noticed.
    

Dawes would not know the technical details. He would know the _shape_ of the operation.

And he would know exactly what kind of man would build something like this.

## 6. SSWG Conclusion

The Ceres incident demonstrates that:

- The drift‑era network could be bypassed by any operator with sufficient technical knowledge and access to mixed‑jurisdiction hardware.
    
- Jurisdictional routing was not a security measure; it was a political convenience.
    
- Metadata classification was the single point of failure for both suppression and propagation.
    
- A unified emergency signaling standard is required to prevent both silent failure and unauthorized replication.
    

This appendix supports the creation of:

- **RFC A: Inter‑Governmental Emergency Hotline Protocol**
    
- **RFC B: Ship‑to‑Ship Emergency and Surrender Signaling**
    

Both are considered mandatory to prevent recurrence of drift‑era failures.
