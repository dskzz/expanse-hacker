# The Nine Blocs: Internal Governance

Status: new file, 2026-11-25. `earth-political-structure.md` names the
nine blocs and how they relate to each other and the UN; this file is
the deferred follow-up — "basic fleshing out... based on historical
patterns," per the standing instruction, not an exhaustive constitution
for each. Each bloc gets its own real institutional ancestor rather than
a shared template, the same principle used everywhere else in this
corpus: free structure, original texture. Each entry also notes how the
bloc fills its seven UN caucus seats, its share of Committee of the
Whole delegates, and — where it says something interesting about the
bloc's own political character — who it's likely to send to the Joint
Military Council (`earth-political-structure.md`).

## The Atlantic Zone

The most internally heterogeneous bloc by a wide margin, and its
governance shows it: no single national government, but a **Zone
Council** of heads of government/state from every member nation,
operating by negotiated consensus with a **rotating Presidency** — the
real EU Council's own structure, just scaled up to include North
America and the WEOG outliers. The seven UN seats are apportioned by a
population/economic-weight formula, weighted to protect smaller members
from pure erasure — in practice the US and the major European powers
(Germany, France, the UK) hold most of them, with Australia, New
Zealand, and Israel holding token, marginal representation, the same
structural position they hold within the Zone itself as a whole.

The defining internal tension is the one real geopolitics already
argues about today: whether the Zone is genuinely post-national
integration or just the United States wearing an EU-shaped coalition,
and which half of that argument the rotating Presidency actually serves
in any given year. A bloc this comfortable with civilian-led technocracy
most plausibly sends a civilian career diplomat to the Joint Military
Council, not a soldier.

**Zone-level opposition:** this is also where the Earth-wide
Sovereignty League (`earth-political-structure.md`) is most at home as a genuine
domestic movement, not just a cross-bloc abstraction — nativist,
anti-further-integration, strongest exactly where the declining
industrial cities already sit (`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`).

## The African Union

The real, currently-existing AU, given centuries to actually become what
its own "Agenda 2063" only aspired to: genuine federal union, not loose
coordination. Governance follows the real AU's own existing shape,
matured rather than reinvented — an **Assembly of Heads of State** as
the supreme body, a continental **Commission** as the executive, and a
directly elected **Pan-African Parliament** giving it more democratic
legitimacy than most of the other blocs can claim. Formal equality among
member states coexists with real, informal dominance by the continent's
heaviest economies (the Nigeria/Egypt/South Africa/Ethiopia tier, by
real present-day weight) — the same pattern as Germany inside the EU or
Brazil inside Patria Grande (below), a regional heavyweight leading a
formally-equal union without ever needing to say so.

Internally, the old regional blocs the real AU was built from —
West African, East African, North African, Southern African — never
fully dissolve into one undifferentiated whole; continental unity
coexists with real regional caucusing underneath it, the same way the
Atlantic Zone's own member nations never stopped being nations. This is
also the bloc the Proportionality Caucus's own case rests hardest on
(`earth-political-structure.md`) — real demographic and economic weight,
no veto seat to show for it.

**Zone-level opposition:** the old regional blocs are also where
domestic opposition actually organizes — regionalist coalitions
pushing for real devolved authority rather than deeper continental
centralization. Call the leading one **the Lagos Compact**, West
African regionalists pushing back against Addis Ababa's own
continental center — direct real ancestor: ECOWAS-style
sub-regionalism never actually disappearing just because the AU
matured.

## Bharat

India's own actual federal parliamentary democracy, carried forward
essentially unchanged in structure: a **Prime Minister** heading the
government, a bicameral legislature, a **President** as ceremonial head
of state. The most genuinely competitive, electorally contested
politics of any veto power — real multi-party contestation, real
elections that actually change the governing coalition, in sharp
contrast to the Zhongguo bloc and the Eurasian Union below. That has a
real, mechanical consequence the other veto powers don't share: Bharat's
UN delegation is appointed by whichever coalition currently governs, so
its own international posture can genuinely shift after a domestic
election in a way China's or Russia's never will. Its seven UN seats
and its slice of the Committee of the Whole are filled by the sitting
government's own appointment process, same as the rest of its foreign
service.

Real center-state tension — India's own states wanting more autonomy
from New Delhi — persists at bloc scale, an old fault line that didn't
close just because Bharat's external stature grew. And Bharat's own
still-unresolved relationship to the Proportionality Caucus
(`earth-political-structure.md`) is as much a question about its
domestic politics as its foreign policy: a different governing coalition
could plausibly answer it differently than the one that won the veto
seat in the first place.

**Zone-level opposition:** real India's actual opposition politics are
genuinely multi-party and highly contested, and the sharpest current
real axis is agricultural — the real 2020–21 Indian farmers' protests
are recent enough to extrapolate directly. Call the opposition
coalition **the Kisan Front** ("kisan," a real term already in use in
actual Indian politics, meaning farmer) — pushing back on whatever
food-security policy the ruling coalition pursues, a direct, current,
well-grounded fit for the agricultural-policy axis already flagged
above.

## The Zhongguo-descended bloc

A unitary, centralized state projected forward, standalone by its own
real weight rather than folded into a coalition — a **paramount
leader** and a small inner council hold real power, with a **National
People's Congress**-style body providing formal, non-contesting
legitimacy rather than genuine electoral check. No internal
coalition-building the way the Atlantic Zone or Bharat need: the UN
delegation and the Committee of the Whole's national allotment are
simply appointed by the central government, with no real domestic
contestation behind the choice. Managing distant, historically distinct
frontier regions is a recurring administrative theme for a state this
large and this centralized, handled the way any unitary centralized
power manages such regions rather than through federal devolution.

The live, textured tension here isn't internal so much as external and
already established: the resentment over Bharat's veto seat
(`earth-political-structure.md`, `flashpoints.md`) sits closer to the
surface in Zhongguo's own politics than in the Atlantic Zone's or the
Eurasian Union's, precisely because this bloc's whole self-conception
runs on not sharing status it considers rightfully its own. Given that
same political culture, this is the bloc most plausibly sending a
retired general rather than a civilian to the Joint Military Council.

**Zone-level opposition:** kept deliberately light and structural
rather than invented — real organized public opposition doesn't
really exist given the centralized character. What passes for
opposition here is quiet elite factionalism inside the leadership
itself over economic priorities, not a public movement with a name.

## The Three Seas-descended bloc

The loosest governance of all nine, deliberately — the real,
currently-existing Three Seas Initiative was never a federal project,
just a summit-based cooperation framework, and this bloc stays
genuinely close to that shape rather than hardening into one. No strong
central executive: an annually **rotating host presidency** convenes
summits, and common positions require real unanimity among member
states rather than majority vote, the same consensus-bound character as
the real Initiative today. That looseness has a direct consequence for
how it shows up at the UN — its seven caucus delegates and its
Committee of the Whole contingent can arrive less unified, more visibly
improvised, than the delegations of blocs with a real central
government behind them.

The already-established tension is the sharpest of any bloc's: formally
excluded from the veto club and a natural Proportionality Caucus
sympathizer on paper, but culturally and historically closer to the
Atlantic Zone's own orientation than to the African Union or Patria
Grande — a bloc whose formal interest and actual alignment pull in
different directions, with no governance strong enough to resolve that
tension even if it wanted to.

**Zone-level opposition:** no new named movement needed — the
already-established internal split does the work. Sovereignty-League-
aligned Atlantic-leaners on one side, members wanting deeper
Three-Seas-only integration on the other, the same "sharpest swing
case" tension just expressed as domestic politics instead of bloc-
level alignment.

## The Eurasian Union

The real, currently-existing Eurasian Economic Union, matured: formal
equality among member states, administered through a **Supreme Eurasian
Economic Council** operating nominally by consensus — and real,
Russia-anchored dominance underneath that formal equality, the same
structural honesty the rest of this corpus applies everywhere else
("formal legitimacy vs. real power"). Russia's own government fills the
bulk of the seven UN seats and leads the Committee of the Whole
delegation; junior members hold real but limited input, enough to be
genuine rather than purely decorative.

The live tension is the real one already visible in the actual Eurasian
Economic Union today: junior members' own genuine wariness of Russian
dominance, persisting even while their own economies stay bound to it —
close enough to need the arrangement, uneasy enough never to fully trust
it.

**Zone-level opposition:** that same junior-member wariness, given an
actual name and coalition — **the Steppe Accord**, Kazakhstan/
Armenia/Kyrgyzstan-anchored, pushing for real autonomy within the
Union rather than exit.

## Patria Grande

South America's own Mercosur-descended union, Brazil-anchored by the
same real demographic and economic logic that makes Brazil the region's
heaviest power today. Governance follows Mercosur's own real shape: a
formally **rotating pro-tempore presidency** among member states,
masking Brazil's de facto leadership regardless of whose turn it
formally is — structurally identical to the African Union's own
heaviest-economy dynamic above, a pattern this corpus is comfortable
reusing because real regional unions actually produce it independently,
more than once.

The defining internal friction is real regional rivalry for leadership
status — Argentina's own historical competition with Brazil for
regional primacy is the obvious real-world ancestor — persisting under
a formal structure that insists every member is equal, the same quiet
mismatch between paper and practice running through nearly every bloc
in this list.

**Zone-level opposition:** real Latin American politics' actual
populist tradition, Peronism above all, is the direct ancestor. Call
it **the Patria Front** — an ironic name, claiming to be the true
voice of Bolívar's founding dream against what it frames as Brazilian
dominance dressed up as regional unity, Buenos Aires-anchored. A
second, sharper grievance now aims outward rather than inward: the
Atlantic Zone's own informal Good Neighbor Understanding
(`earth-political-structure.md`), the Patria Front's own name for
exactly the neo-colonial continuation Bolívar's dream was supposed to
escape in the first place.

**A genuine flagship piece of orbital infrastructure, finally, and an
Ecuadorian one rather than Brazil's own:** the Equatorial Bulk Export
Program's mass-driver complex at Chimborazo
(`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`), real, specific geography (the
equatorial bulge making Chimborazo's summit the farthest point from
Earth's center of any mountain) doing work no other bloc's own
territory could. A rare point of bloc-wide pride that doesn't run
through Brazil at all, even if the program itself is a joint UN
venture rather than Patria Grande's alone.

## ASEAN and Others

Southeast Asia's own real "ASEAN Way" carried forward essentially
intact: decisions by **consensus, not majority vote**, strict
non-interference in member states' domestic affairs, and an annually
**rotating chairmanship**. That consensus requirement is a genuine
double edge — it's exactly what lets a bloc this diverse function at
all without one member dominating the rest, and exactly what makes it
slow and occasionally paralyzed on anything genuinely contentious, the
same real criticism leveled at actual ASEAN today.

Turkey, bolted on as the "Others" of this era
(`earth-political-structure.md`), attends under the same consensus
rules as every other member but fits them awkwardly — a non-Southeast-
Asian state whose own historical weight and Ottoman legacy sit outside
everything the ASEAN Way was actually built to manage. Its seat on the
seven-delegate caucus is real, but its actual standing inside the
bloc's own consensus culture is still the unresolved question
`earth-political-structure.md` already flags.

**Zone-level opposition:** the ASEAN Way's own consensus requirement
is a real double edge already flagged above — its domestic opposition
is a reformist push for faster, majority-vote decision-making,
frustrated with consensus paralysis, plus Turkey's own domestic
factions genuinely split on whether membership is worth it at all,
echoing its already-unresolved status.

## The OIC-descended bloc

Built on the real, currently-existing Organisation of Islamic
Cooperation, and genuinely tense by the same real-world inheritance:
the Sunni-Shia divide and the Iran-Saudi rivalry don't dissolve on
contact with a shared institution any more than they have in reality.
Leadership plausibly rotates, uneasily, between Iran and Saudi Arabia —
echoing the real OIC's own practice of rotating its Secretary-General
nominally among member states while Saudi Arabia's hosting of the
organization's actual headquarters gives it outsized real influence
regardless of whose turn it is.

**Pakistan's own position makes it the bloc's natural broker rather
than a junior partner to either rival** — Sunni-majority but not Arab,
nuclear-armed, with its own long history of balancing Saudi funding
against a wary, separate relationship with Iran. That gives Pakistan
real leverage inside the bloc precisely because it isn't a party to the
Iran-Saudi rivalry itself, the same kind of indispensable-mediator role
that made Bharat admissible as a veto power despite itself being
outside the OIC bloc's own sphere. The bloc's seven UN delegates and
its Committee of the Whole contingent are a genuine negotiation among
all five members (plus Bangladesh and Afghanistan), not a Saudi or
Iranian rubber stamp — which is exactly why forming one unified
position is real, recurring work rather than a formality.

**Zone-level opposition:** deliberately not sectarian — not the right
place to invent something that could read as commentary on real
religious politics. The sharpest live internal split instead tracks
the bloc's own signature issue: a "diversify now" reformist faction
arguing the bloc should invest in the fusion economy rather than
resist it, breaking from the dominant fusion-era-anxiety posture
(`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`) along economic lines, not
religious ones.

## Still open

  UN delegation head, or Joint Military Council seat-holder yet — all
  added to `people-pass-queue.md`.
- How contested or stable each bloc's own rotating-presidency or
  appointment mechanism actually is in practice (a crisis year, a
  disputed succession) isn't worked out for any of the nine.
- Whether any bloc's internal regional sub-structure (the Atlantic
  Zone's EU-core-vs-North-America tension, the African Union's old
  regional blocs, Patria Grande's Argentina/Brazil rivalry) has ever
  actually produced a real internal crisis — left open, the same way
  `earth-political-structure.md` leaves the planetary-level fissures
  theoretical rather than historical.
