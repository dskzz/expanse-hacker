# Space Law Compacts

Status: new file, 2026-11-25. Grew out of a long question-and-answer
pass working through "what real maritime-law principles would Sol's
spacefaring powers have already adapted by now" — not one invented
mechanic, a whole real body of law (rescue, salvage, collision rules,
general average, limitation of liability, insurance, piracy,
nonproliferation, transit rights, taxation) ported over compact by
compact, each with a real-world ancestor doing the actual design work.
Companion to `earth-political-structure.md`, which already hosts
Additional Protocol IV (Outer Space) and the UN's own military-teeth
framework; this file is where the rest of that body of law actually
lives.

## The Sol Tripartite Process

The standing mechanism behind every compact below except the oldest
one (the Good Samaritan Compact predates it; see its own entry): **a
UN resolution, built through its own Committee of the Whole/caucus
machinery (`earth-political-structure.md`), proposes treaty text,
which then becomes the basis for actual negotiation among exactly
three parties — the UN (Earth's unified voice), Mars (MDR), and OSL.**
Not a four-party or open process: three seats, by design. "The good
Belters" is real, slightly patronizing Earth/Mars-side slang for OSL
specifically, one OSL itself may not love given what it implies about
ASA by contrast. **ASA's exclusion from the Process is structural, not
incidental** — it doesn't occupy a seat because it doesn't seek the
recognition that comes with having one, which is exactly why ASA's
compliance status keeps turning up unsettled across every compact
below rather than being a repeated coincidence. Protocol IV's own
already-described signing (Mars and OSL as independent treaty parties)
already followed this shape; this is just naming it as the standing
procedure rather than one-off diplomacy.

## The Good Samaritan Compact of 2112

Predates the Sol Tripartite Process entirely — pure Earth/UN-era law,
centuries before Mars or the Belt were independent political actors,
later simply inherited and ratified by both as they came into being.
The real model is a near-exact echo of 1912: the actual Titanic
disaster is why binding distress-response law exists in reality at
all (SOLAS, 1914), specifically because the SS *Californian* was close
enough to see the Titanic's distress rockets and didn't respond. 2112
is the same beat, two centuries later, in space — worth treating as a
deliberate echo rather than coincidence.

**The duty covers persons, not only vessels** — real UNCLOS Article 98
language already extends the obligation to "any person found at sea in
danger of being lost," not just ships, so a lone spacer adrift on an
emergency beacon with no vessel at all is already covered by this
compact exactly as written; no separate treaty needed for that case.

**The founding incident:** the colony transport *Amaranth*, Mars-bound,
suffers a reactor/life-support casualty mid-transit and transmits
genuine distress. A nearby independent bulk hauler — remembered to
history only by its own ship's name, the *Ondine* — detects the
signal, and its master calculates that diverting would blow a
penalty-laden delivery contract and burn propellant margin they
weren't confident they could spare. The *Ondine*'s own log officially
flags the signal "unconfirmed, low-confidence" and holds course. By
the time a second, more distant ship answers, most of the *Amaranth*'s
complement is dead. The *Ondine*'s own flight recorder survives and
gets subpoenaed — this corpus's "logs are testimony, not truth" theme
(`os-lineages.md`) applying two centuries before SolNet even existed —
showing the crew knew the signal was real and chose the schedule
anyway. Public outrage forces the UN to codify a hard legal duty
rather than a professional courtesy. The hauler's owning company is
bankrupted by the resulting liability; "doing an *Ondine*" is a clean,
usable in-universe idiom for coldly calculating a life against a
schedule and choosing the schedule.

**Exceptions, as laid down by law** (real UNCLOS/SOLAS language, plus
one genuinely harder, space-specific addition):

- **The serious-danger exception** (real, direct): no duty if
  rendering assistance would seriously endanger the assisting ship,
  its crew, or passengers.
- **The margin exception** (new — the one place space law has to be
  strictly harder than maritime law ever was): no duty if diverting
  would leave the assisting vessel without sufficient propellant,
  power, or life-support reserve to secure its own safe return. Open
  ocean, a diversion is almost always survivable with enough fuel and
  patience; in space it can be an irreversible commitment with no
  do-over. This is the exception the *Ondine*'s master should have
  been able to invoke honestly and didn't — the record showed they had
  the margin and lied about it.
- **The refusal exception** (real, direct): a master can't have
  rescue forced on them — if the distressed vessel's own commander
  explicitly refuses assistance, the duty lapses. A real, usable hook
  given this corpus's politics: a vessel would rather die than accept
  rescue from a political rival.
- **The bad-faith exception, and the exploit it was written for:** no
  duty if the responding master reasonably believes the distress
  signal itself is fraudulent — faking distress to lure a law-abiding
  ship into range is a known, old piracy tactic, and this is the same
  exploit category as `docs/rfc-companions/RFC 2300`'s "spoofed beacon
  puzzle" (a fake `ProxBeacon` mimicking a trusted console). A distress
  call near ASA-contested space is genuinely, legally ambiguous to
  respond to, not just dramatically tense — the law itself says so.
- **Wartime modification, layered on top rather than replacing it:**
  Additional Protocol IV (Outer Space, `earth-political-structure.md`)
  extends this same peacetime norm into active conflict specifically,
  which is why the two documents read as one continuous legal lineage
  two centuries apart, not coincidentally similar rules.

## Salvage Law

A genuinely different legal animal from the Compact above — property
law, not rescue law, and the clean real distinction is worth keeping:
a **derelict** (abandoned, no one aboard, no reasonable hope of
return) can be claimed by whoever recovers it, with a right to a
**salvage reward** — a lien against the vessel's own value, not
outright ownership by default. The governing real principle, kept
intact rather than reinvented: **"no cure, no pay"** — a salvor is
owed nothing unless the attempt actually succeeds, a clean incentive
structure. A damaged-but-not-abandoned vessel (owner reachable, not
consenting) is a different case — **voluntary salvage** requires the
owner's actual consent or genuine unreachability, not finders-keepers
on anything merely adrift. Disputes run through the International
Court's own admiralty jurisdiction (`earth-political-structure.md`)
rather than a separate court.

## Interplanetary Rules of the Road

A direct adaptation of real COLREGS (collision-avoidance "rules of the
road" at sea): right-of-way and priority rules for traffic and docking
approaches. The universal rule is set once at the Sol Tripartite
level; purely local implementation is left to whoever actually runs
traffic control at a given location — the same "narrow waist, local
realization" pattern already governing addressing (`os-lineages.md`
§12). Jovian Traffic Control (`callisto.md`) is the obvious,
already-built local enforcer, not a new institution.

## The General Average Compact

A direct adaptation of real general-average doctrine: when an
emergency forces jettisoning cargo or burning deep into reserve
propellant to save the ship, the resulting loss is shared
proportionally among everyone who benefited from the ship's
survival — ship owner, cargo owners, anyone with a stake — rather than
falling entirely on whoever's specific property got sacrificed. A
natural fit given how much this corpus already treats fuel and mass
budgets as a real, binding constraint (the Good Samaritan Compact's
own margin exception, the Loadframe's power-budget logic,
`equipment-codex.md`).

## The Limitation of Liability Compact

A direct adaptation of a real, genuinely controversial maritime
doctrine: a shipowner can cap their liability for an accident at the
vessel's own value rather than face unlimited exposure. Kept
consistent with everything else cynical already built into this
corpus: pushed hardest by the same old-money shipping interests (the
Sol Industrial Trust, Hauptmann & Voskuijlen, `corporations.md`) that
already buy weak enforcement elsewhere, at real cost to whoever
actually gets hurt and ends up under-compensated. Makes the
Marine Insurance framework below economically viable in the first
place — insurers won't underwrite genuinely unlimited liability, so
the cap and the insurance market are mutually reinforcing, the same
way they co-evolved in reality.

## The Interplanetary Marine Insurance Framework

No single, uniform market — each major power's own institutional
character (`os-lineages.md`, `earth-blocs.md`) produces a genuinely
different model, not a shared template:

- **Earth/UN space:** a real Lloyd's-of-London-style private
  underwriting market for hull insurance, old-money-dominated
  (Hauptmann & Voskuijlen prominent among underwriters), alongside
  **P&I-club-style mutual associations** — real Protection & Indemnity
  Clubs, owned by their own shipowner-members — for third-party
  liability coverage, which the Limitation of Liability Compact above
  makes actuarially viable at all. **RAP/SPERB conformance
  certification becomes a real insurability precondition** here, the
  same way real marine insurers already require classification-society
  certification (Lloyd's Register, DNV, ABS) before writing a policy —
  giving RAP/SPERB's own certification real economic teeth through the
  market rather than through law, consistent with SolNet's whole
  access-gating-not-coercion character (`os-lineages.md` §12). **Claims
  verification is a genuinely hacking-relevant problem**: proving what
  actually happened to a lost ship runs straight into "logs are
  testimony, not truth" (`os-lineages.md`) — insurance fraud, disputed
  telemetry, and forged loss reports are real, mechanically live
  content here, not flavor.
- **Mars:** a state-backed, MDR-administered indemnity system rather
  than a private market — rigorous on paper, genuinely expensive and
  infrequent to actually claim against, the same "expensive ceremony"
  character already established for Mars's capability-fork root model
  (`os-lineages.md` §4).
- **The Belt (OSL side):** informal, mutual, union-based risk-pooling
  rather than a formal market at all — a direct extension of
  Scrapshell's own quorum/union culture (`os-lineages.md` §3) into
  economic risk-sharing, not a separate invention.
- **ASA:** no formal coverage of any kind. A ship operating under
  ASA's own banner is simply uninsurable by any of the above standards
  — a real, standing economic cost to that allegiance, not just a
  reputational one.

## Anti-Piracy and Universal Jurisdiction

A direct adaptation of a real, old doctrine: pirates as *hostis
humani generis* ("enemy of all mankind"), subject to universal
jurisdiction — any party may act against a confirmed pirate regardless
of flag or treaty standing. Gives a sharp, legally real lens for a
question this corpus has otherwise left as tone rather than law: is a
given ASA action piracy under this compact (anyone may act against
it), or protected/ambiguous belligerent conduct under Protocol IV's
own combat rules? That ambiguity is a real, standing tension, not a
loose end waiting to be tidied up.

## Flag-State Jurisdiction ("the law of the flag")

Not a new compact — naming a principle that's already been quietly
doing real work throughout this corpus: a vessel is subject to the
law of whichever jurisdiction's flag it flies, regardless of its
actual location. This is the real legal basis underneath Doris's
entire existence (`corporations.md`) and underneath LocationChain
addressing generally — worth stating outright rather than leaving
implicit.

## The Interplanetary Nuclear Non-Proliferation Agreement

**All nine Earth blocs are already nuclear-armed by the time any such
treaty is conceivable** — a grim, long-settled fact of life
predating the treaty by a long way, not something it tries to undo.
This is a deliberate, worth-naming divergence from the real NPT: in
reality the UNSC's P5 and the NPT's recognized nuclear-weapon states
are the same historical accident (whoever built the bomb first in
WWII). Here, three centuries of further proliferation mean nuclear
status and veto status have **decoupled** — Bharat's veto seat came
from funding/tech leverage (`earth-political-structure.md`), not
nukes, and the other five non-veto blocs are just as nuclear-armed as
the four veto powers. Universal possession among Earth's own blocs
means nuclear weapons stopped being a status marker there long ago.

**So the treaty's real, specific work matches the real 1967 Outer
Space Treaty's actual Article IV** — which never tried to
un-proliferate Earth either, only to keep the new domain of space
itself free of the problem: a ban on orbital basing and weapons on
celestial bodies, plus a bar on proliferation to *new* actors — Mars,
the Belt, corporate or non-sovereign claims. Negotiated through the
Sol Tripartite Process, with **Mars and OSL both voluntarily
committing to non-nuclear status as a negotiated term, not an imposed
one** — a real diplomatic win for both (treated as serious enough to
negotiate with, not just regulated), and genuinely costless for Mars
doctrinally, since Ballista-class precision already does the
"controlled, proportionate force" job nukes are bad at anyway.

**The real teeth: a UN Nuclear Safeguards Directorate**, running
IAEA-style inspections of fuel-cycle facilities — directly relevant
given this corpus's own live fusion-fuel economy (Thisbe Station's
helium-3 discovery, Europa's deuterium, Lunar depletion driving the
whole scramble, `earth-resources-and-pressures.md`). Thisbe's own
cooperative, now sitting on a major discovery, is a live, concrete
candidate for mandatory inspection, and its founding-trauma-driven
distrust of anything UN-affiliated (`thisbe-station.md`) makes
compliance a real, standing friction point. A confirmed diversion
triggers the UN's own real, usable lever — orbital embargo
(`earth-political-structure.md`) — tying enforcement to machinery
already established.

**ASA's status is deliberately left open**, consistent with its
exclusion from the Sol Tripartite Process generally: whether it has
ever sought or acquired a crude fissile capability is a sharp, dark,
standing question, not a resolved fact either way.

Placement: roughly the 2140s, a few decades after the Good Samaritan
Compact, once Mars had enough independent industrial capacity to make
proliferation a real rather than hypothetical concern. Adjustable.

## The Interstation Tax Compact

A direct adaptation of the real 2021 global minimum corporate tax
deal (138+ countries agreeing to a 15% floor specifically to stop
profit-shifting into tax havens) — and it lands squarely on a seam
this corpus already built: **Doris**, the thin, barely-staffed
flag-of-convenience jurisdiction (`corporations.md`).

**Captured by design, not just weakly enforced** — the sharper,
more cynical cousin of the Torture Convention's genuine-if-imperfect
enforcement (`earth-political-structure.md`). The Compact sets a real,
binding minimum tax floor and information-sharing obligation, clearing
the same high-consensus threshold the Torture Convention did, for the
same reputational-cost reason. But Doris signs — refusing would draw
exactly the scrutiny its whole business model depends on avoiding —
and its "thin, barely-staffed local board" has no real capacity to
audit anything, so signing costs it nothing and changes nothing. That
isn't a loophole nobody noticed; it's the loophole everyone who
matters wants left open.

**Who pushed for it, and who quietly ensures it stays weak:** the
blocs with the most acute revenue need — the African Union, Patria
Grande, Bharat (`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`'s own demographic
pressures all cut this way) — pushed hardest. The Atlantic Zone
couldn't be the lone holdout without real reputational cost, so it
signed too, while its own old money has the strongest incentive of
anyone to keep enforcement toothless — the same funding-leverage
mechanism already established buying weak enforcement here instead of
influence directly. **The UN Monetary Authority**
(`earth-political-structure.md`, with its own capital-weighted voting
hierarchy) is the natural monitoring body, consistent with its real
IMF/OECD-adjacent role. **This directly deepens the Rockline Compact's
existing suspicion of the Sol Industrial Trust** (`corporations.md`,
`flashpoints.md`): the Compact's suspicion that Oberth Extraction never
gets undercut now has a concrete mechanism worth naming outright —
Trust-affiliated structures routed through Doris, technically clean
under the Tax Compact's letter, gutting it in spirit.

Placement: much more recent than the other compacts — roughly the
2320s, contemporaneous with the Frontier Opportunity Program.
Adjustable.

## The Freedom of Transit Compact

A direct adaptation of real UNCLOS "innocent passage"/"freedom of
navigation" doctrine — and, same as in reality, **loaded with
loopholes by design**, not merely in practice. Negotiated through the
Sol Tripartite Process once Jovian trade (JDC, JTA) made orbital
corridors and trade routes valuable enough to actually fight over;
placed in the same modern era as the Tax Compact, roughly the 2300s–
2310s, adjustable.

**The loopholes, each a real-world-grounded ambiguity rather than an
invented flaw:**

- **"Innocent passage" is judged by whoever controls the corridor.**
  Real doctrine requires passage to be genuinely non-threatening, but
  the compact never settles who decides that — a polity controlling a
  transit corridor can unilaterally declare a given ship's passage
  "non-innocent" (suspected smuggling, suspected ASA affiliation,
  suspected weapons transport) and deny or delay transit without real
  due process, a legal-sounding pretext for what's really commercial
  or political harassment.
- **The security exception is the same lever already used to
  weaponize "neutral" standards.** Pretextual "security" claims
  (radiation advisories, traffic congestion, an active JTC advisory
  zone) can block a competitor's shipping while waving a favored
  company through — structurally the same move as the Atlantic Zone's
  own technical-standards manipulation in UN procurement
  (`earth-political-structure.md`), just applied to transit instead of
  contracts.
- **"Reasonable" transit tolls aren't defined, and chokepoints
  matter.** Real precedent (Suez, Panama) allows reasonable passage
  fees; "reasonable" is exactly as vague here. Armstrong City/Luna —
  already established as the literal gateway between Earth's own
  networks and the rest of SolNet (`os-lineages.md` §12) — is the
  obvious, structurally real chokepoint where a punitive toll dressed
  as a reasonable transit charge could quietly become a major lever,
  economic and political at once.
- **Flag-of-convenience gaming through Doris.** A ship can reflag
  through Doris specifically to claim transit rights or protections
  its true operator's actual bloc identity wouldn't get — the law of
  the flag (above) doing for transit exactly what it already does for
  taxation, giving Doris yet another practical function stacked on
  the same "formal compliance, zero real constraint" foundation.
- **The embargo carve-out is the biggest loophole, written into the
  text on purpose.** The Compact has to exempt UN-authorized embargoes
  (`earth-political-structure.md`'s real, usable lever) from the
  general freedom-of-transit guarantee — which means whoever can get
  an embargo authorized through the same high-consensus threshold
  effectively gets a *legal* override of freedom of transit for anyone
  they want. In practice, fights over authorizing or opposing an
  embargo are fights over who gets to suspend this compact for whom.
