# Slayton Construction Yards

Status: locked, 2026-11-24. Eighth full station write-up, and the
corpus's deliberate replacement for Expanse's Tycho Station — built
with an actual reasoned history behind every piece, rather than
Tycho's own underexplained jump from "spun up some habitat asteroids"
to "runs the system's biggest shipyard," and without the
plausibility gap of a backer building its flagship project somewhere
UN protection couldn't fully reach and then being surprised when
someone else takes it.

## Siting: a real Kirkwood gap, not just an empty spot

Sited in or near one of the asteroid belt's real **Kirkwood gaps** —
genuine orbital-resonance zones (with Jupiter) that have been
gravitationally swept relatively clear of debris over billions of
years, at specific, well-known distances comfortably past Mars and
before Jupiter. Not hand-waved emptiness: a physically real reason
this particular stretch of the belt is safe for large-scale
construction, and the same clearing effect that makes it safe also
makes it a natural shipping corridor — ships crossing the belt
already prefer routing through the cleared gap over threading dense
clusters, so traffic concentrates there on its own. Shipyard and
transshipment point aren't two functions bolted together; one grows
directly out of the other.

A happy accident, not a planned one: the gap sits squarely on the
natural path between the inner system and the outer system both
ways, so cargo flows through in both directions — inner-to-outer and
outer-to-inner alike cross paths here, not just belt-internal
traffic. Nobody designed it as a crossroads; the orbital mechanics
made it one.

## Founding: an early, experimental reach for direct resource access

Opened early — one of the system's pioneering deep-Belt construction
ventures, built specifically to take direct advantage of Belt
resources on-site rather than shipping raw material all the way back
to Earth, Luna, or Mars and shipping finished construction back out
again. An experimental proof of concept for "can this much
infrastructure actually be built this far out," not a mature
operation from day one.

**Why it ended up largely corporate — practical reach, not ideology.**
This far out, this early, UN administrative jurisdiction simply
couldn't be effectively projected and enforced at that distance; it
wasn't a deliberate choice by anyone to build somewhere UN law
couldn't fully reach, it was a basic fact of early deep-system
logistics and communication. Corporations with their own direct
operational presence out there, pursuing their own resource and
construction interests, were the ones who actually built and ran it.
That's the honest version of the gap Expanse's own Tycho/Nauvoo
history never quite closes.

**Cultural drift followed naturally, not by design.** Being this deep
in system, far from inner-system cultural and administrative
influence, the yard's actual workforce — generation after
generation — drifted progressively more Belter in character over
time regardless of its formal corporate ownership structure. The
people who actually live and work somewhere shape its culture more
than a distant corporate HQ ever does. By the time Elias Voss takes
over management (~2318, `os-lineages.md`'s Frontier Opportunity
Program entry), the yard is already a long-established, deeply
Belter-cultured institution, not a blank slate he's building from
scratch.

## Ownership and management today

**Lodestar Shipyards** (`corporations.md`) owns the facility — this
deep corporate history is why, not a recent development. **Elias
Voss** manages it day-to-day. The UN's relationship to the yard runs
through the Frontier Opportunity Program as a funded joint program,
not direct ownership — real leverage and influence, but not
unilateral authority to remove Lodestar's own chosen manager, and
removing Voss specifically would carry real political cost given his
redemption-figure history, the same "can't move against a sympathetic
figure without reopening an old wound" logic protecting Thisbe's
cooperative from a UN crackdown. Two separate, real reasons Voss's
position holds, not one assumed immunity.
