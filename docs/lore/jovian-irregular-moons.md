# The Irregular Moon Families: Jupiter's Own Mini-Belts

Status: locked, 2026-11-25. Deliberately a single, light-touch
treatment covering all four families at once — none of these
individual bodies earns a Peale- or Europa-level writeup, and that's
the point. The real value here is the cluster itself, not any one
rock in it.

## Real facts: debris families, not random scatter

Beyond the four Galileans, Jupiter has dozens of small, irregular
moons — real, captured bodies grouped into four genuine families by
shared orbital characteristics, each group almost certainly the
fragments of one larger captured body that broke apart. The same
"asteroid family" logic already used for Vesta's Rheasilvia debris
and Thisbe's regional cluster (`asteroid-bases.md`, `thisbe-station.md`),
just orbiting Jupiter instead of scattered loose through the main
belt:

- **The Himalia group** — the closest in, and distinctively
  **prograde** (the normal orbital direction) unlike the other three,
  real evidence of a gentler or earlier capture history. Himalia
  itself (~150 km) anchors it; Elara, Lysithea, and Leda trail behind,
  each smaller.
- **The Ananke group** — retrograde, farther out, a tight debris
  family from one ancient breakup.
- **The Carme group** — retrograde, similar distance, another tight
  fragment family.
- **The Pasiphae group** — the most distant and loosely scattered of
  the four, sitting right at the edge of Jupiter's actual
  gravitational influence (its Hill sphere) — the frontier of the
  frontier, genuinely the last thing still gravitationally "Jupiter's"
  before interplanetary space takes over.

## The mini-belt difference: actual neighbors

This is the real distinction from the main asteroid belt, not just a
label: the Belt's rocks are genuinely isolated from each other across
real distance, but each of these families clusters tightly at one
orbital distance from Jupiter. Ships and stations sharing a family
have real neighbors, real mutual support, real local traffic density
— something the Belt's own hardest-luck settlements (VPH,
`asteroid-bases.md`) never had. Radiation here is negligible too,
far outside even Callisto's safe zone, so the only real constraint
left is the same one the Belt has always had: these rocks are tiny,
with negligible natural gravity, so anything built here needs Cole
Spindle Works-style spin-habitat construction regardless of which
family it's in.

## Who's actually out here, and why

**Functionally ungoverned, not formally independent — a meaningful
difference.** Nobody in the Jovian sovereignty crisis (`callisto.md`)
is fighting over these families; they're too scattered, too small
individually, and too far from Concord's own administrative reach to
be worth anyone's real attention. That's not freedom by declaration,
it's freedom by neglect — the same mechanism, just with genuinely
better real estate than the Belt's own neglected places ever had.

**A natural landing spot for Belt diaspora.** VPH's displaced ASA
sympathizers and Psyche's Core refugees (`asteroid-bases.md`,
`psyche.md`) have real reason to end up here specifically: genuinely
independent-minded politics, nobody effectively governing them day to
day, but real neighbors in the same family and real proximity to
Ganymede's wealth and Callisto's safety net if they ever need it —
strictly better than anything the actual Belt offered them. That
gives the Jovian system's own independence sentiment (the United
Moons, `callisto.md`) an actual demographic root out here, not just
Concord's comparatively privileged administrators wanting out from
under a charter.

## Open threads

- No named settlements, companies, or figures in any family —
  deliberately left as collective texture rather than specific
  places, consistent with the "no special treatment" scope here.
- Whether any single family has developed its own distinct identity
  beyond "which orbital distance you live at" isn't decided.
- Whether the JDC, UN, MDR, or OSL has ever bothered asserting formal
  jurisdiction out here, or whether "nobody's bothered yet" simply
  continues indefinitely — open, and worth revisiting only if a
  scenario actually needs it to matter.
