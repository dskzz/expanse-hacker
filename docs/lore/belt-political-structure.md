# Belt Political Structure (replaces "OPA")

Status: locked naming decision, 2026-11-20. Replaces the placeholder use
of "OPA" (Outer Planets Alliance) throughout earlier notes/RFCs with an
original structure — not a 1:1 rename, a genuine split into two rival
organizations plus a shared founding document.

## The Anti-Solar Accord

The founding charter/mutual-aid agreement signed by early Belt stations —
the shared origin point both successor organizations below claim to be
the legitimate heir of. Not an organization itself; a text. Both
factions invoke it, and disagree about what it actually requires.

## Outer System League (OSL)

The moderate wing. Diplomatic, seeks formal recognition from the UN and
MDR rather than confrontation. The one that actually shows up in
cross-domain negotiation contexts. Probably the default "this is the
Belt's political domain" reference wherever a document needs one
without taking a side in the OSL/ASA rivalry.

## Anti-Solar Alliance (ASA)

The hardline wing. More defiant, more willing to act unilaterally, more
paramilitary-coded. Claims the League sold out what the Accord actually
meant. "Anti-Solar" works as both a literal direction (away from the
sun) and a political identity (against inner-system/establishment
control) — the double meaning is intentional and free, not borrowed.

## Relationship to OPRA

OPRA (Operational Relay Authority) stays explicitly separate from both —
a neutral technical/comms standards body, not a political one, that
both OSL and ASA still have to use regardless of their rivalry. OPRA was
never going to rebrand into either of these; that idea is retired.

## Still open

- Exactly when the split happened relative to the rest of the corpus's
  timeline (the Seven Day War / the Massacre at Thisbe Station / the
  "trickling out" RFC era) isn't pinned down yet. Matters for the eventual rename sweep:
  an existing "OPA" reference in an older/earlier-era document might
  need to become "the Accord" or an unsplit precursor movement rather
  than OSL or ASA specifically, depending on when that document is
  meant to be set.
- The actual corpus-wide rename sweep (72 mentions of bare "OPA" across
  24 files, per the session's count) has not been done. Unlike the
  MCRN→MDR/MRN sweep, this one needs a per-mention judgment call, not a
  mechanical find-and-replace, because of the OSL/ASA split above.
