# Belt Political Structure (replaces "OPA")

Status: locked naming decision, 2026-11-20. Replaces the placeholder use
of "OPA" (Outer Planets Alliance) throughout earlier notes/RFCs with an
original structure — not a 1:1 rename, a genuine split into two rival
organizations plus a shared founding document.

## The Anti-Solar Accord

The founding charter/mutual-aid agreement signed by early Belt stations —
the shared origin point both successor organizations below claim to be
the legitimate heir of. Not an organization itself; a text. Both
factions invoke it, and disagree about what it actually requires.

## Outer System League (OSL)

The moderate wing. Diplomatic, seeks formal recognition from the UN and
MDR rather than confrontation. The one that actually shows up in
cross-domain negotiation contexts. Probably the default "this is the
Belt's political domain" reference wherever a document needs one
without taking a side in the OSL/ASA rivalry.

**What that recognition actually is: Permanent Observer status at
the UN** (`earth-political-structure.md`) — a genuine seat and a real
diplomatic mission, no vote on binding resolutions. Co-option, not
absorption, and a weaker deal than it could be specifically because
OSL's real leverage (the Rockline Compact's suspicions, the Belt's
own informal technical backdoor at Ganymede, `ganymede-condominium.md`)
has never been unified the way a single bloc's leverage is — split
between OSL and ASA instead. That fragmentation is exactly the
mechanical reason OSL settled for observer status rather than real
power, and exactly what ASA means by "sold out."

## Anti-Solar Alliance (ASA)

The hardline wing. More defiant, more willing to act unilaterally, more
paramilitary-coded. Claims the League sold out what the Accord actually
meant. "Anti-Solar" works as both a literal direction (away from the
sun) and a political identity (against inner-system/establishment
control) — the double meaning is intentional and free, not borrowed.

## OSL's own seat: nominal at Ceres, actually the Circuit

OSL's formal diplomatic address is Ceres — its UN Permanent Observer
mission's own correspondence routes there, and that's the only sense
in which Ceres is OSL's "capital." The real governing body never
actually settles there, or anywhere else: it runs a deliberate,
recurring circuit, meeting at a different notable rock roughly once a
month, occasionally extending all the way to the Jovian system. Belters
just call it **the Circuit** — plain, functional naming, same register
as everything else OSL does.

**Why not just settle at Ceres, given it's the Belt's biggest hub:**
because Ceres's own local government is compromised — Hetman Piast,
"owned lock, stock, and barrel by Earth" (`ceres.md`), personally
leveraged by Hauptmann & Voskuijlen. OSL physically refusing to
headquarter itself there, while still using Ceres as its nominal
address for Earth's own diplomatic convenience, is a continuous, quiet
rebuke of Piast's claimed centrality — not a neutral administrative
choice. It's the same rotating-seat logic already used twice elsewhere
in this corpus (the Three Seas bloc's rotating host presidency, Patria
Grande's rotating pro-tempore presidency, `earth-blocs.md`), just
applied to physical location instead of only to who holds the chair —
a real, recognized way smaller or more fractious bodies keep any one
member's own turf from becoming the de facto center of gravity.

**Regular stops worth naming, without pinning the whole itinerary:**
Vesta, deliberately and repeatedly, despite — or because of — being
ASA's own stronghold (`asteroid-bases.md`): OSL insisting on including
it reads as refusing to concede the rock entirely to ASA's own
narrative. Thisbe Station, periodically, carrying exactly the
founding-trauma weight you'd expect. And sometimes Jupiter — most
plausibly Callisto — which means OSL's investment in the Jovian
sovereignty crisis, the Europa Allocation Council, and the Concord
Settlement (`callisto.md`, `europa.md`) isn't abstract diplomatic
interest; its own itinerant leadership physically shows up there as a
normal part of how it governs.

## A third axis: "unclaimed"

OSL and ASA are a binary, but not every station or person in the
Belt actually sits inside it — and unlike Earth, where a third
ideological axis meant standing up new organizations (the Sovereignty
League, the Free Commerce Alliance, `earth-political-structure.md`),
the consistent move here is different, because it has to be: an
organized third faction would contradict the thing it's trying to
express. **"Unclaimed"** is real salvage-law vocabulary
(`space-law-compacts.md`) borrowed for political standing instead of
property — a station or person who flies neither League nor Alliance
colors, by choice or by circumstance. No faction's flag, for better
and worse: no formal backing from either side if trouble comes, and
no obligations to either side either.

**Deliberately not a standing organization**, consistent with the
same "ancap in networking" character already established for SolNet
itself (`os-lineages.md` §12) — a political structure claiming to
speak for the unclaimed would defeat the point. It can still coalesce
informally around one moment or one station without ever becoming
permanent, the same way SolNet's own minimal, unenforceable standards
never needed a standing authority to work.

**Two genuinely different flavors of unclaimed, not one:** chosen
unclaimed status, a deliberate rejection of both factions' claims to
speak for the Belt — and unclaimed by simple remove, where neither
faction has ever bothered to show up in the first place. **Davida**
(`asteroid-bases.md`) is the clean example of the second kind:
already established as "genuinely ordinary... no distinctive
political character," which is exactly what unclaimed-by-neglect
actually looks like on the ground — not a principled rejection of
OSL and ASA, just a rock nobody's politics ever found a reason to
reach.

**Correction worth stating plainly: Pallas is not that example, and
an earlier pass here got this wrong before catching it.** Pallas,
Vesta, and Hygiea (VPH, `asteroid-bases.md`) are all already
established as ASA territory, not neutral ground — Vesta organized
and angry, Pallas quiet and self-reliant ("arguably the closest thing
in the Belt to genuine ASA self-governance in practice"), Hygiea
quietest and most overlooked. Pallas's own real orbital inclination
(~34° off the main belt's plane) is already doing real work in
`asteroid-bases.md` — it's *why* Pallas never drew flagship
investment and became the stepchild of the three, not evidence that
neither faction reached it. Worth being honest about the correction
rather than quietly fixing it: VPH stays ASA's territory in three
real flavors; Davida does the unclaimed-by-neglect work instead.

**Placed together, the Belt's own station-level map reads as a real
spread rather than one flat "the Belt opposes Earth" story:** Ceres
(Imara Oduya's "Ceres for Belters," OSL-aligned reform, `ceres.md`),
Vesta (organized worker councils, ASA's angriest cell,
`asteroid-bases.md`), Pallas and Hygiea (quieter ASA territory, two
different flavors of the same alignment), and Davida (genuinely
unclaimed, neither faction's politics ever found a reason to land
there) — real variation across the Belt rather than one politics
repeated at every station.

## Two threshold ceremonies, in opposite directions

Real modern navies still keep genuine threshold-crossing traditions
today, more than one of them — the Equator ("Crossing the Line"),
the International Date Line ("Golden Dragon"), the Arctic Circle
("Blue Nose"). The real function underneath all of them is marking a
notable first relative to your own normal frame of reference, which
is exactly why Belters don't have one for entering the Belt itself —
it's home, not a threshold. But the same logic, run in both
directions away from that home, gives them two real ones instead.

**"Down the Well"** — a Belter's first real trip into a planetary
gravity well (Earth or Mars), genuinely risky rather than festive:
sudden full-gravity exposure after a life adapted to low or no
gravity is a real, documented spaceflight-medicine danger, not mere
discomfort (`equipment-codex.md`'s Loadframe entry). Consistent with
Belter culture's own dry, hardship-normalizing register (the "[X] be"
tautology, `os-lineages.md` §11), this reads as a grim badge of
having survived an ordeal, not a party.

**"Past the Rocks"** — plain, Belter-coded naming rather than a
borrowed nautical term, playing on literally living among asteroids:
leaving them behind for the sparser, lonelier reach toward Jupiter's
system and beyond — already established elsewhere as a real step
change in isolation (Lassell Relay's own four-hour light-lag,
`lassell-relay.md`). Still a real "further than normal" milestone
even for someone the Belt itself never threshold-marks.

**Two more ceremonies sit outside this Belt-specific pair, each tied
to a home territory rather than the Belt** — Earth's own "Leaving the
Cradle" beyond Lunar orbit (`armstrong-city.md`) and Mars's own
"Beyond the Moons" past Phobos/Deimos (`mars-political-structure.md`)
— plus two genuinely Sol-wide ones tied to a specific extreme rather
than anyone's home: Diving the Furnace at Mercury's perihelion
(`mercury.md`) and **Saturn's Gate**, crossing the real heliocentric
distance of Saturn's own orbit into the deep outer system — the
innermost and outermost extremes of the whole set, not built out
further until Saturn itself gets its own pass.

## Relationship to OPRA

OPRA (Operational Relay Authority) stays explicitly separate from both —
a neutral technical/comms standards body, not a political one, that
both OSL and ASA still have to use regardless of their rivalry. OPRA was
never going to rebrand into either of these; that idea is retired.
