# Earth Political Structure (adds "the New International")

Status: locked naming decision, 2026-11-21; massively expanded
2026-11-25 with the nine-bloc structure, UN governance, and the
UN's actual military "teeth," then again the same day with the
Committee of the Whole, money/lobbying/corruption, the Fleet Staff/
Joint Military Council split, Mars's peer-power footing,
procurement/geographic-return contracting, and the four specialized
agencies (World Court, Monetary Authority, Public Health Directorate,
Commons and Environment Directorate) and their own domain-split bite,
then again with the UN Human Rights Council (one broad mandate, the
Committee Against Torture one treaty-specific committee under it),
Additional Protocol IV (Outer Space) extending real Geneva Convention
tradition off-world with genuinely enforceable bite given the same
domain split, and a note on each bloc's own CA root/trust-domain
fragmentation under Earthstock (`os-lineages.md` §12).
Each bloc's own internal governance, and Earth's demographic/resource
pressures, now have their own files: `earth-blocs.md` and
`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`. Earth had the same "one org speaks for
everyone" thinness the Belt had before OSL/ASA and Mars had before
the Commonwealth of Elysium: the UN with total, unchallenged control
and no organized opposition of any kind. Real populations that large
essentially never look like that. This also closes a genuine gap the
corpus had sat on without naming it: 20th/21st-century socialist and
communist political tradition had simply vanished from the setting's
future-Earth politics, with no in-universe reason given.

## The United Nations (UN)

Earth's governing body, kept as-is — real, generic, already-confirmed-
safe terminology (same bucket as "tightbeam" or the Moon's real
Armstrong crater). Earth's political establishment: institutional,
bureaucratic, the Earthstock OS lineage's own "verbose, ceremony-heavy,
over-trusting of its own PKI" culture (`os-lineages.md` §2) is this
establishment's natural technical expression. Each bloc plausibly runs
its own CA root under that shared Earthstock lineage — distros of one
lineage, not nine separate networks — with cross-certification between
any two blocs' roots tracking their actual diplomatic relationship
rather than any technical need; `os-lineages.md` §12 covers the
underlying paradigm this is one instance of.

## The nine blocs

Earth's actual day-to-day governance happens here, not at the UN —
real, distinct institutional ancestors for each, not a uniform
"Zone" template:

- **The Atlantic Zone** — Western Europe, North America, and (per
  the real UN's own historical WEOG quirk) Australia, New Zealand,
  and Israel folded in as geographic outliers with nowhere else to
  go. "Zone" is a deliberate fossil: a naming habit from an era when
  the West assumed it was the default and didn't need a prouder
  name, kept long after the relative demographic and economic weight
  it once justified has shifted elsewhere.
- **The African Union** — the real, currently-existing AU, given
  centuries to actually achieve what its real "Agenda 2063" framework
  only aspires to today: genuine federal union, not loose
  coordination. The name stays exactly as it is now specifically
  because an institution this old and this real doesn't need
  rebranding, only deepening.
- **Bharat** — India's own real, constitutionally-used traditional
  name for itself, not an externally-imposed label. India-anchored
  rather than a shared South Asian coalition — real SAARC-style
  equal coalitions keep failing in reality over India-Pakistan
  tension, so India leads its own bloc under its own name instead.
- **[Zhongguo-descended bloc]** — built on China's own real self-
  name, "Zhongguo" (中国, "Middle Kingdom"/"Central State"). Standalone
  rather than folded into a broader East Asian coalition, given
  China's real demographic and economic weight on its own.
- **[Three Seas-descended bloc]** — Eastern Europe, explicitly not
  Russia, descended from the real, currently-existing Three Seas
  Initiative (a real Baltic/Adriatic/Black Sea cooperation framework
  already partly conceived as a counterweight to Russian influence).
  A believable extrapolation of real, current EU internal East-West
  tension deepening into a full institutional split.
- **The Eurasian Union** — Russia-led, descended from the real,
  currently-existing Eurasian Economic Union (Russia, Belarus,
  Kazakhstan, Armenia, Kyrgyzstan). Fits neither the Atlantic Zone
  nor Eastern Europe nor China's bloc — consistent with Russia's own
  real "Eurasianism" self-conception as neither fully European nor
  fully Asian, but its own civilization.
- **Patria Grande** — South America, Brazil-anchored given its real
  demographic and economic dominance in the region, descended from
  the real Mercosur trade bloc. Named for Simón Bolívar's genuine,
  never-fully-realized historical dream of a unified Spanish-American
  federation — a real dream, finally realized centuries late, not an
  invented founding myth.
- **ASEAN and Others** — Southeast Asia, plus Turkey as the "Others"
  of this era: the same real bureaucratic pattern that produced WEOG's
  own "and Others" clause recurring a second time, because
  institutions reuse their own awkward compromises rather than invent
  fresh ones. Turkey's membership is a deliberate, somewhat desperate
  pivot after real EU accession talks went nowhere for decades and
  its own Ottoman legacy complicates any comfortable fit with an
  Iran/Saudi-anchored bloc — genuinely still contested, not a settled,
  comfortable fit on either side.
- **[OIC-descended bloc]** — Iran, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan
  (Pakistan getting real regional weight here instead of being
  Bharat's leftover), plus Bangladesh and Afghanistan as natural
  fits, descended from the real, currently-existing Organisation of
  Islamic Cooperation. Genuinely tense from day one — the real
  Sunni-Shia divide and Iran-Saudi rivalry don't dissolve just
  because the bloc exists, the same register of internal friction as
  Mariner Valley versus Elysium or OSL versus ASA.

**Still genuinely unresolved, not papered over:** Turkey's actual
membership status in ASEAN and Others; North Africa's dual identity
between the African Union and the OIC bloc (the same real ambiguity
today's actual AU and OIC both just live with); Mongolia and North
Korea as true leftovers nobody's bothered to resolve. Each bloc's own
detailed internal governance is covered separately in
`earth-blocs.md`.

## Representation: appointed, not directly elected

No one votes for a UN representative directly. Each bloc selects its
own UN delegation through whatever internal process its own
government actually uses — the same logic as the United States'
pre-1913 Senate, chosen by state legislatures rather than by popular
vote, just applied at planetary scale. This makes the UN's own
internal workings resemble a **caucus system**: closed-door,
negotiated, horse-traded among a small number of bloc delegations,
not an open floor contested by public campaigning — the same real
shape as actual UN Security Council or EU Council negotiations today,
not an invented mechanism.

**Fixed and equal, not proportional — the formal injustice the
Proportionality Caucus exists to fight.** Each of the nine blocs
sends exactly seven delegates to the General Assembly-style body (63
total), regardless of population. Bharat and the African Union, with
real populations in the billions, hold the same seven seats as the
Three Seas bloc. If representation were already proportional, there'd
be nothing for the Proportionality Caucus to push for — the flat
inequality is the grievance, made concrete rather than abstract.
Voting happens only among these appointed delegates; there is no
popular, planet-wide "assembly of the whole."

## The Committee of the Whole: where the pointless stuff happens, on purpose

A separate, much larger body from the 63-delegate caucus above, and
deliberately so. Convened at New York (below), it's where each bloc's
same seven caucus delegates sit *alongside* up to three additional
delegates each individual member nation sends in its own right —
dozens of nations across nine blocs, each entitled to its own voice,
producing a body that can run into the hundreds. The name is real
parliamentary terminology, not invented: a legislature's full
membership convening as a single committee rather than through its
normal smaller structure.

This is where the UN does the real-world UN's actual, recognizable
day job — non-binding resolutions, declarations, grandstanding
speeches, the "pointless stupid stuff" every real international body
produces in volume. That's not wasted motion. It's standing
infrastructure for the one moment it actually matters: when a genuine
crisis forces real consensus (the OPRA-shaped pattern already
established), the Committee of the Whole is the only body already
built at planetary scale to produce and legitimize it fast. The
machinery that looks useless in peacetime is exactly what gets used
when an emergency needs the whole system's visible buy-in, not just
the four veto powers' private agreement.

## Permanent veto power: four seats, not nine

**Four blocs hold a legacy veto: the Atlantic Zone, the Eurasian
Union, the Zhongguo-descended bloc, and Bharat.** The first three
map directly onto the real UN Security Council's actual P5
composition once the US/UK/France collapse into one Atlantic Zone —
not an invention, a direct extrapolation. Bharat's seat is the fourth
and newest, won rather than given (below). The remaining five blocs —
the African Union, the Three Seas-descended bloc, Patria Grande, ASEAN
and Others, and the OIC-descended bloc — hold no veto at all, despite
several of them having real populations far larger than at least one
veto-holder. That mismatch between formal power and real demographic
weight is deliberate, not an oversight — it's the same shape of
injustice already running through MDR's Martian Congress
malapportionment and Elysium's ceremonial-vs-real Consular seat, just
at planetary scale.

**Why Bharat, and why now: co-option, not fairness.** Real
institutional power doesn't dilute itself voluntarily. Bharat's
admission came from concentrated leverage, not moral argument: its
economy growing large enough that its assessed UN funding
contribution rivals or exceeds the Eurasian Union's own, and —
extrapolated directly from India's real, current dominance in IT and
software services — Bharat becoming the indispensable steward of
some critical network or administrative computing backbone the other
blocs have grown dependent on, the same shape of leverage as
Taiwan's real "silicon shield" today. Real international-relations
logic: accommodate a rising power into the club before it becomes a
revisionist threat outside it. The existing three didn't choose this
out of fairness — they chose it because the alternative (an
increasingly indispensable, increasingly resentful outside power
drifting toward the Proportionality Caucus) was more dangerous than
controlling the risk by absorbing it. Real history backs the "least
objectionable" framing too: India's actual Non-Aligned Movement
legacy and its real reputation for pursuing its own interests without
the kind of confrontations the US, Russia, and China have each had
with one another made it the rising power none of the three existing
veto-holders saw as *their own* primary rival.

**The Zhongguo bloc minds this far more than the other two, and
still does.** Real India-China regional rivalry — actual border
disputes, actual competition for Asian strategic leadership — means
China experiences India's elevation as a direct, personal demotion in
exactly the arena it cares about most, in a way the Atlantic Zone and
Eurasian Union never felt as sharply. Zhongguo was the last and most
reluctant holdout, forced into accepting a fourth veto seat rather
than choosing it, and the resentment never actually went away.

## Loyal opposition: the Proportionality Caucus

The New International (below) is the revolutionary answer; this is
the loyal one — a faction operating entirely within the UN's own
accepted bounds, pushing to reapportion real voting and
representational weight to actually match the demographic and
economic shift already underway. Not clandestine, not revolutionary —
just the formal, working-the-system version of the same underlying
grievance, the same relationship Elysium's own devolution politics
have to outright separatism (`mars-political-structure.md`): real,
standing friction that hasn't tipped into rupture.

**Bharat's admission complicates the Caucus rather than vindicating
it.** For years Bharat was the Caucus's clearest example — real,
massive demographic weight, formally excluded all the same. Now that
Bharat holds a veto seat, it's an insider with every reason to defend
the system that finally let it in, not necessarily an ally to the
blocs still waiting, chief among them the African Union. Whether
Bharat still backs the Caucus's cause out of principle, or quietly
treats its own admission as proof the system works and stops pushing
further, is a live, unresolved question about Bharat's own character
— not decided, and worth deciding deliberately rather than by
default.

## Natural fissures and alliances

**The four veto powers are a coalition of convenience, not
friendship.** Real historical and ongoing rivalry runs through every
pair of them — Atlantic Zone/Eurasian Union, Atlantic Zone/Zhongguo,
Eurasian Union/Zhongguo, and now Zhongguo/Bharat as the newest and
rawest — yet all four close ranks to protect the veto system itself,
the same way the real Security Council's P5 behave today: disagree
on everything substantive, agree completely that the club shouldn't
grow any further or lose its own privileges.

**The other five blocs are the Proportionality Caucus's natural
base, but not a clean one.** The African Union, the Three Seas-
descended bloc, Patria Grande, ASEAN and Others, and the OIC-descended
bloc share the same formal grievance, without sharing much else. The
OIC bloc's own internal Iran-Saudi tension doesn't pause for this
cause; the real India-Pakistan tension that already complicates OIC-
Bharat relations doesn't go away just because both might otherwise
sympathize with reapportionment. The Three Seas bloc is the sharpest
swing case of all: formally excluded from the veto club (real
sympathy for reform), but culturally and historically closer to the
Atlantic Zone's own orientation than to the African Union or Patria
Grande — torn between its formal interest and its actual alignment.

## The Good Neighbor Understanding

**The real precedent isn't the Marshall Plan — it's the Monroe
Doctrine, softened into the Good Neighbor Policy's own friendlier
branding**, both genuine, real US foreign-policy history: the 1823
Monroe Doctrine's blunt "this hemisphere is ours, stay out," later
dressed up in the 1930s as cooperative-sounding rhetoric while the
underlying primacy stayed intact. The same "friendly name for a
power-political reality" move running through everything else in this
corpus (the Mariner Valley Caucus, the Sol Industrial Trust's own
name).

**Generalized across all four veto powers, not kept as one bloc's
privilege** — the same symmetry every other great-power mechanic here
already applies evenly: an informal, deliberately *unwritten* norm,
not a signed treaty, since formally admitting to carving up spheres of
influence would be exactly the kind of diplomatically radioactive
admission the Sol Industrial Trust's own suspected-but-unproven
collusion already shows this corpus avoids putting on paper. Each
veto power's own traditional regional primacy goes informally
unchallenged by the other three: the Atlantic Zone over Patria Grande
(the direct Monroe Doctrine descent), the Eurasian Union over its own
traditional near-abroad, the Zhongguo bloc over its own regional
periphery. **Bharat's own equivalent claim is deliberately left
open** rather than forced to parallel the other three — it chose not
to lead a SAARC-style regional bloc in the first place
(`earth-political-structure.md`, above), so whether it has a
comparable sphere at all is a live, unanswered question, not a clean
fourth parallel.

**This gives the Patria Front a second, sharper grievance, not a
repeated one** (`earth-blocs.md`): already resentful of Brazilian
dominance dressed up as regional unity, it can now also target the
Good Neighbor Understanding directly — the exact neo-colonial
continuation Bolívar's real Patria Grande dream was supposed to escape
in the first place, aimed at the Atlantic Zone this time instead of
Brazil.

## Command structure: the Fleet Staff and the Joint Military Council

The Secretary-General is not the Fleet's commander-in-chief —
concentrating that authority in one civilian office would recreate
exactly the single point of failure the whole rogue-command design
(below) exists to avoid. Real operational and authorization authority
splits across two deliberately different bodies instead: a small
professional command staff that actually runs the Fleet, and a larger
civilian body that decides whether it's allowed to act.

**The Fleet Staff** holds real operational command, and stays small
and professional on purpose: a **Home Fleet Admiral** (Earth-system
defense), a **Frontier Fleet Admiral** (everything further out — the
same home-fleet/distant-fleet split real historical navies have used,
borrowed as free generic structure, not any one franchise's specific
invention), a **Marine Commandant** commanding the Delhi Protocol's
treaty-capped expeditionary forces, and a **Merchant Marine liaison**
representing civilian shipping's own real stake in Fleet policy. These
four run day-to-day operations. They do not decide, on their own
authority, to use force.

**The Joint Military Council** is the civilian oversight and consent
body sitting above the Fleet Staff: one seat per bloc, nine total,
mirroring the General Assembly-style body's own equal structure
regardless of veto status. Nothing beyond routine operations — any
real use of force, any blockade — happens without the Council's
consensus-gated authorization (below). Each bloc chooses its own
seat-holder by its own process, civilian by default, and that choice
is itself characterization: a bloc with a strong civilian-control
tradition sends a career civilian, while the Zhongguo-descended bloc,
reflecting its own more militarized political culture, might just as
plausibly send a retired general instead. The Secretary-General sits
over neither body directly — a civilian political coordinator, not a
military one, the same real principle of civilian control of the
military most actual governments already run on.

## Intelligence and counterintelligence: the Directorate of Security Services

**Police power stays with the blocs; this is the space-only
exception, the same domain split running through everything else
here.** Earth-domestic intelligence and policing are each bloc's own
business, not detailed further — the UN's own service, the **UN
Directorate of Security Services (DSS)**, has jurisdiction only over
off-world and interplanetary matters, consistent with the UN's
authority generally being real and exercised in space while staying
toothless on Earth. The name itself is deliberately dry and
administrative — "Security Directorate" reads as facilities/IT
security as readily as it reads as an intelligence service, which is
itself the same "formal legitimacy" camouflage running through every
other institution in this corpus. Spacers call it **the Quiet
Service**, an open secret rather than a real one — nobody's fooled
about what it does, same as how no one's ever really fooled about
what a real intelligence agency does either.

**Why it exists, in order of how load-bearing each reason is:**

- **Counterintelligence over the Fleet's own authorization systems is
  the single most important job on paper.** The rogue-command
  vulnerability (above) — a stale, cached, or long-lived-trust
  exception letting one compromised credential bypass nine blocs'
  worth of political consensus — is exactly the kind of insider
  compromise DSS's counterintelligence wing exists to catch. Whether
  it actually can is a different question: the same compartmentalized,
  low-visibility nature that lets an intelligence service function at
  all is exactly what would let DSS itself become the vulnerability it
  was built to prevent, rather than its solution. Deliberately left
  open, consistent with how the rogue-command hook has been treated
  everywhere else — nothing confirms DSS has ever been compromised, or
  has ever been the one to compromise it.
- **The covert counterpart to the Nuclear Safeguards Directorate's
  overt inspections** (`space-law-compacts.md`) — the real IAEA/
  intelligence-agency split: declared inspections catch what a
  facility admits to; covert human and technical intelligence is what
  actually catches what it doesn't. Thisbe Station's own helium-3
  program and ASA's fissile-material status are the two live
  questions DSS has the clearest institutional reason to actually
  care about answering.
- **Resolving, covertly, what the Sol Tripartite Process structurally
  can't** (`space-law-compacts.md`): ASA's compound legal ambiguity
  across Protocol IV, the Anti-Piracy Compact, and the
  Non-Proliferation Agreement all share the same shape — nobody with
  standing has ever had to decide. DSS is the body actually trying to
  find out, since the diplomatic and legal machinery structurally
  can't reach a party that holds no seat in it.
- **A secondary interest in off-world corporate activity** — the
  Rockline Compact's suspicion of the Sol Industrial Trust
  (`corporations.md`, `flashpoints.md`) is fundamentally about
  interplanetary commerce (Belt mining), not bloc-domestic regulation,
  putting it at least partly inside DSS's actual remit even though
  nobody would officially describe economic counterintelligence as
  its job.

**Captured the same way everything else in this corpus is.** DSS
nominally serves all nine blocs equally; its real staffing and
leadership lean disproportionately toward the four veto powers,
especially the Atlantic Zone — the same old institutional capacity
that makes Earthstock "closest to the original" (`os-lineages.md`)
carries over directly into who actually staffs a dry, technical-
sounding security service first. That gives the Proportionality
Caucus (above) a live, concrete grievance here too, distinct from
funding and representation: an intelligence service funded by all
nine blocs but answering, in practice, mostly to four of them.

Oversight runs nominally through the Secretary-General and the Joint
Military Council — but covert operations are structurally harder for
any nine-bloc consensus mechanism to actually verify than a Fleet
action is, giving DSS more real unchecked latitude in practice than
the Fleet officially has, even though both sit under the same
formal chain. Mars plausibly runs its own peer intelligence service out
of Admiralty House's own command structure, watching DSS exactly as
warily as DSS watches Mars; not built out further than that yet.

## Mars: a peer power, not a tenth bloc

MDR is not one of the nine blocs and never has been — it isn't an
Earth constituency to fold in, it's a separate sovereign power with
its own government, its own military, and its own seat at no one
else's table. Its relationship to the UN runs through ordinary
sovereign-to-sovereign diplomacy — treaties and ambassadors — rather
than through any bloc's internal caucus process, the Committee of the
Whole, or the Joint Military Council. The MRN's own command structure
(Admiralty House, the Ballista-class, `mars-political-structure.md`,
`ship-class-codex.md`) is entirely separate from the UN Fleet and
answers to no UN body at all. Given Mars's real demonstrated military
and economic power, it plausibly holds something more substantial in
practice than the Belt's Permanent Observer status — but that's not
pinned down further than this yet.

## Selecting, and removing, the Secretary-General

Chosen by the caucus of delegates, with any one of the four veto
powers able to block a candidate outright — the same practical shape
as real Security Council influence over the actual UN Secretary-
General's selection today. **Removal requires clearing the exact same
bar**, which is the load-bearing detail: a Secretary-General who has
secured even one veto power's quiet backing is nearly unremovable
through legitimate channels. That symmetry is precisely what makes
the rogue-leader scenario (below) dangerous rather than merely
theoretical — legitimate removal failing is the reason any real
opposition to a rogue Secretary-General would have to look for a
technical answer instead of a political one.

**The Secretary-General's own cabinet**, real Under-Secretary-General-
style deputy posts below the top office, is informally expected to
balance across the nine blocs for legitimacy — the same real practice
the actual UN already follows today. Not detailed further than that
yet; individual deputy portfolios are a future task.

## Money, lobbying, and corruption

**Lobbying exists, but it has no UN-level campaign to target, since no
one runs for a UN seat.** It aims at two things instead: shaping which
individuals a bloc's own internal process actually appoints as
delegates, and influencing those delegates' negotiating positions once
seated — Lattimer & Cross's own established business (`corporations.md`)
operates at exactly this level. Real electoral campaign finance, where
it exists at all, lives one level down, inside each bloc's own
domestic politics, and its character varies with that bloc's own
political system rather than following one planetary pattern:

- **The Atlantic Zone** runs the most money-saturated democratic
  politics of the nine, a direct extension of its established old-
  money character — real PAC-style and corporate-donation patterns,
  the same old capital that already buys it disproportionate UN
  funding leverage (below) buying domestic influence too.
- **Bharat** runs real, massively expensive elections — extrapolated
  directly from India's actual present-day elections, already among
  the costliest in the world in absolute terms — but through scale
  and patronage networks more than concentrated corporate money.
- **The Zhongguo-descended bloc and the Eurasian Union**, both far
  less competitively electoral, see money matter through elite and
  patronage channels instead of public campaign finance — state-
  directed and oligarch-adjacent rather than vote-buying in the
  Western sense.
- The remaining blocs (African Union, Three Seas, Patria Grande, ASEAN
  and Others, the OIC bloc) each plausibly have their own distinct,
  real-grounded character here too, not yet detailed.

**The UN is genuinely, substantially corrupt — the mundane,
bureaucratic kind, not a cartoon.** It splits cleanly by the same two
tiers that already split ceremony from power (below): New York's
ceremonial layer runs on lower-stakes, almost petty corruption —
sinecure appointments, expense-account abuse, blue-ribbon committees
used for calculated delay as often as genuine inquiry. New Delhi's
real-power layer runs on higher-stakes, far better-hidden corruption —
the same category as the Sol Industrial Trust's own suspected
collusion (`corporations.md`), and old money's funding leverage
(below) buying influence no formal vote grants, legally and in plain
sight.

## Other UN departments

Kept light rather than fully built out — only what's load-bearing for
threads already in motion: a **Peacekeeping/Expeditionary Forces**
department overseeing the Delhi Protocol's treaty-capped ground units
(below); an **Off-World Development** department formalizing the
Frontier Opportunity Program and Ceres's MFN arrangement
(`ceres.md`); and a **Conformance and Standards** oversight body,
distinct from OPRA — OPRA stays the neutral technical standards body
everyone has to use regardless of politics, this is the UN's own
compliance-auditing arm checking RAP/SPERB adherence.

Two more, both real-world-grounded and both load-bearing rather than
decorative: the **UN Spaceguard Directorate**, named directly after
the real program of the same name (itself borrowed by actual
astronomers from Arthur C. Clarke's fiction before becoming the real
term of art), runs Earth/cislunar near-object detection — genuinely
dual-purpose, since in a setting where kinetic bombardment is already
the de facto WMD (above), distinguishing a natural impact threat from
a deliberate one is exactly the same detection problem. The **Jovian
Sentinel Survey** (`callisto.md`) is a later, distinct regional body
under Concord's own umbrella, not a Spaceguard branch office, but
explicitly modeled on Spaceguard's own methods — credit without
collapsing the two into one bureaucracy. **Mars's own already-flagged
missing early-warning capability** (`mars-political-structure.md`'s
"Still open") means MDR plausibly leans on Spaceguard data it doesn't
generate itself — a real, standing dependency and a point of genuine
strategic vulnerability given Earth-Mars history, not just an
engineering gap.

The **UN Space Survey Directorate** is the broader scientific
counterpart: mapping, cataloguing, and formally registering newly
surveyed bodies — a real function lifted from the actual 1976
Registration Convention — before anything gets as far as Off-World
Development's own economic arrangements. "Survey discovers,
Development administers" is a clean, deliberate pipeline, the same
division of labor real agencies split between pure science and
commercial administration. That pipeline is also a quiet lever worth
keeping in mind: whoever can influence which bodies the Survey
Directorate prioritizes cataloguing first effectively gatekeeps who
gets first economic mover advantage, without ever needing to touch
the economic decision directly.

More departments can be added as specific scenarios need them rather
than up front.

## The New International

Earth's organized political opposition — genuinely, openly descended
from real 20th/21st-century socialist and communist political
tradition, not hiding that lineage any more than "the UN" hides being
the UN. Political ideology isn't owned IP the way a franchise's
invented proper nouns are; using it directly and specifically, with
real texture instead of a cartoon strawman, is normal and well-
precedented (plenty of hard SF engages directly with capitalism-vs-
socialism tension this way).

**Name:** deliberately revivalist — consciously reclaiming real
historical labor-movement branding (echoing the real "Communist
International"/"the Internationale") the way a modern movement might
reclaim old symbols on purpose, treating 20th-century communism the
way this setting already treats other real Earth history: material to
draw on honestly, not erase.

**Positioning:** organized specifically against corporate concentration
of power — which gives it immediate, concrete relevance to the
Continuity Group cartel (`corporations.md`, pending full writeup) as
the standing, ideologically-motivated check against exactly that kind
of concentration, rather than a vague "anti-establishment" label.

**Status under the UN:** not a tolerated, seated opposition party —
the UN doesn't permit real organized opposition at that scale, so the
New International operates partly openly (as a real, if marginalized,
political movement) and partly clandestinely (as an actual resistance
network when it needs to coordinate, protect members, or move
information the UN would suppress). That clandestine half is the part
with real mechanical relevance to this corpus: it needs exactly the
kind of identity-protection and resistance-network protocols SolNet's
own RFCs already model, giving it a natural, structural reason to
interact with the game's systems rather than existing as pure
background flavor.

**The real distinction between the two oppositions:** the
Proportionality Caucus wants the current system to actually live up
to its own stated principles; the New International has given up on
that system entirely and wants something structurally different. Both
can be right that something's wrong without agreeing on the fix — the
same kind of two-track dissent already running through MDR via the
Mariner Valley Caucus (reformist-adjacent) and Elysium's deeper
devolution question (closer to rupture).

**The New International's own internal fault line.** Every real
revolutionary movement in history splits between a legal/parliamentary
wing willing to work within existing institutions and a radical
underground that views legal participation as compromise — a real,
generic historical pattern, not derivative of any one specific
movement, and this corpus's own version of it has been missing until
now. Kept as live tension inside one organization rather than a clean
split, for now: the open wing increasingly worried the underground's
actions hand the UN a ready pretext for crackdowns; the underground
increasingly views the open wing as a legitimacy-laundering dead end
that will never produce real structural change. Whether it actually
splits is a live, undecided question, not a foregone conclusion.

## The Sovereignty League

The missing right-wing counterpart to the New International — real
nationalist-populist opposition to supranational governance exists
across the political spectrum today (Brexit and the EU's own
euroskeptic parties are the direct real ancestors), and this corpus's
UN had generated only a left-wing reaction until now, which was
lopsided rather than a deliberate choice. The Sovereignty League
targets further UN integration specifically — the rare domestic-
binding mechanic (above) is its favorite example of "dangerous
precedent," the UN Convention Against Torture chief among them.
Strongest in the Atlantic Zone and the Three Seas bloc
(`earth-blocs.md`), where it's also each bloc's own leading domestic
opposition voice, not just an abstraction layered on top.

**The sharp part: its genuine grassroots sentiment happens to serve
the same old-money interest in weak UN enforcement already
established elsewhere** (the Interstation Tax Compact's own capture
via Doris, `space-law-compacts.md`) — not bought off, just unwittingly
useful, a more realistic form of elite capture than outright
astroturfing. Which is also exactly why it's worth distinguishing from
the next one.

## The Free Commerce Alliance

The outright astroturf case, kept deliberately distinct from the
Sovereignty League's genuine populism above — this corpus's rich
corporate-cynicism toolkit (the Sol Industrial Trust, the Rockline
Compact's suspicions, Doris) had never actually been pointed at
"opposition" specifically until now. Reads exactly like a small-
business/consumer-advocacy group, genuinely effective at opposing the
Interstation Tax Compact's enforcement and the Proportionality
Caucus's reapportionment push by dressing both up as government
overreach — secretly funded by the Sol Industrial Trust
(`corporations.md`). A clean, discoverable "is this group what it
claims to be" investigation hook, not a settled reveal — nothing here
confirms the funding trail has ever actually been exposed.

## Where the UN actually sits

Three tiers, each doing a different real job rather than one capital
city performing all of them:

- **New York** — the original, real UN headquarters, kept as the
  ceremonial and historical seat. Prestige without real power, the
  same fossil-naming logic as the Atlantic Zone's own "Zone": kept
  because an institution this old doesn't abandon its own founding
  site, even once the founding site stops being where anything
  actually gets decided. Still home to the UN's blue-ribbon
  committees — real, expert-staffed advisory panels, a natural fit
  for the already-established Earthstock culture (verbose, ceremony-
  heavy institutions, `os-lineages.md` §2). Most of the time these
  exist to look productive while deferring action the high
  domestic-binding threshold makes genuinely hard to clear; on the
  rare occasions a real crisis forces real consensus, a committee's
  recommendation becomes one of the few things that actually goes
  binding.
- **New Delhi** — the real seat of administrative power now,
  reflecting Bharat's own ascendance (`demographics.md`,
  `jovian-irregular-moons.md`'s Vishvakarma naming rationale) directly
  rather than asserting it abstractly. Where the caucus actually
  meets, where the real negotiating happens.
- **Armstrong City, Luna** — the bureaucracy, already established
  (`armstrong-city.md`'s "prestige on Earth, bureaucracy on Luna"
  two-tier geography) — specifically the administrative machinery for
  everything off-world: the Frontier Opportunity Program, RAP/SPERB
  conformance certification, the UN's actual exercised authority in
  space. A third tier, not a contradiction of the first two.

## Funding, and old money's actual leverage

Assessed contributions, proportional to each bloc's economic weight —
the same real mechanism the actual UN uses today. The Atlantic Zone's
relative population share keeps shrinking, but its share of real
wealth (the Trust, Hauptmann & Voskuijlen, old capital that survived
state collapse, `corporations.md`, `demographics.md`) hasn't shrunk
nearly as fast, which means the Atlantic Zone still pays a
disproportionate share of the UN's actual operating budget relative
to its population. That's the real, concrete mechanism behind "old
money retains influence even as its demographic share declines" —
not vague institutional inertia, a specific, checkable fact about who
actually funds the thing, buying leverage no formal vote ever grants
outright.

## Procurement: geographic return, and how the Atlantic Zone actually wins

The UN Fleet, its stations, and its off-world infrastructure all have
to be built by someone, and who gets to build them is real, contested
politics layered on top of real industrial fact
(`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`).

**The baseline mechanism is geographic return**, the same real
principle the actual European Space Agency already runs its own
procurement on: each bloc's share of UN contracts tracks its share of
UN funding (above), not an open, winner-take-all bid. That means
Bharat's own funding weight guarantees it a real, growing contract
share — the same leverage that won it a veto seat applies here too —
and it means the Atlantic Zone's disproportionate funding share
(old money, not population) guarantees it a disproportionate baseline
share as well, before any lobbying even starts.

**Real competition happens around the edges of that baseline, not
against it.** Two levers matter most: **prestige contracts** —
flagship vessels, major stations — aren't bound as tightly to strict
proportional return, so blocs lobby hard (the same Lattimer & Cross-
style channels already established) to win the ones that carry extra
visibility beyond their guaranteed share. And **technical standards**
are the quieter, more effective lever: a bloc with real influence over
the UN's own Conformance and Standards directorate (above) can write a
contract's technical requirements around its own existing industrial
base — a real, well-documented defense-procurement tactic, not an
invented one — without ever needing to argue for its own contractor by
name. **This is specifically how the Atlantic Zone out-competes a
rising Bharati contractor**: not by denying Bharat its own guaranteed
geographic-return share, but by keeping the most advanced, highest-
prestige specifications pinned to a technical bar that only the
Atlantic Zone's own real aerospace-grade manufacturing base
(`earth-resources-and-pressures.md`) can plausibly clear — a soft,
technocratic exclusion dressed as a neutral engineering requirement,
the same "formal legitimacy vs. real power" throughline running
through everything else in this corpus.

## Specialized agencies: nominally independent, structurally tied

Real international bodies — a World Court, a monetary authority, a
public-health directorate, an environmental one — exist here the same
way they exist in the actual UN system today: **formally independent
specialized agencies, not departments the Secretary-General commands
directly**, but tied back into the same funding and bloc politics
everything else here runs on.

- **The International Court** (colloquially "the World Court," the
  same real nickname the actual ICJ already carries) adjudicates
  disputes *between blocs* — trade, resource-commons, treaty
  interpretation — not domestic law inside any one bloc, and not
  individual criminal matters. **Its bite follows the same domain
  split as everything else here.** On Earth, its rulings are advisory
  in practice: binding only as far as the losing bloc's own consent
  extends, the same real toothlessness the actual ICJ has today when
  a major power simply declines to comply. Off-world, where the UN's
  authority is already real and exercised (RAP/SPERB, the Frontier
  Opportunity Program), the same Court's rulings carry genuine,
  enforceable weight — one institution, two very different levels of
  real power depending entirely on which domain a dispute falls in.
- **The UN Monetary Authority** handles inter-bloc currency stability
  and development lending — the real IMF/World Bank's actual role,
  under a different name. Its governing vote is **weighted by capital
  contribution, not by the one-bloc-one-seat logic of the UN caucus**
  — the same real IMF/World Bank practice today — which means its own
  internal power ranking doesn't mirror the veto-power list at all.
  The Atlantic Zone's old money commands even more concentrated
  influence here than in the general caucus; Patria Grande or ASEAN
  and Others, holding no veto seat, could plausibly out-rank a veto
  power inside this one institution if their own capital contribution
  warrants it — a genuinely different hierarchy sitting right next to
  the political one, not a restatement of it.
- **The UN Public Health Directorate**, modeled on the real WHO,
  coordinates disease response and medical research standards —
  genuinely load-bearing given how many sealed habitats and closed
  biospheres this corpus already has (Ganymede's agriculture, Venus's
  wellness resort, every station's own life support) — but
  coordinates by consent, same as the real WHO: it can recommend, not
  command, inside any bloc's own borders.
- **The UN Commons and Environment Directorate**, modeled on the real
  UNEP, tracks and reports on cross-border pollution and shared-
  resource disputes (air, water, anything that doesn't respect a
  bloc's own boundary) but — consistent with Earth staying toothless
  absent the high-consensus-threshold bar above — has no independent
  enforcement power of its own; it's a forum and a registry, and real
  cross-border pollution disputes get settled the way everything else
  on Earth does, bloc-to-bloc negotiation, with the Directorate's own
  data as the shared starting point both sides at least have to argue
  against.

**None of these are the UN caucus or the Secretary-General acting
directly** — all four keep their own separate governance, the same
real "specialized agency" fiction the actual UN system runs on today,
even while every one of them stays reachable by the same funding
leverage and bloc lobbying already established everywhere else in
this file.

## Domain-split authority: toothless on Earth, federal in space

The UN's constitutional character depends entirely on which domain
is in question — not a blend, a hard split:

**On Earth, closest to the real, current UN.** Domestic policy is a
bloc matter. The UN doesn't command it directly — it coordinates,
negotiates, and sets treaties the blocs have to actually agree to.
**But this is a political limit, not a structural one** — the real
EU shows exactly how this can change: when enough member states
genuinely agree, EU law becomes real, binding law overriding domestic
sovereignty. The UN's own caucus system can do the same, gated behind
a deliberately high consensus threshold (well above the simple-
majority bar for routine coordination, closer to unanimity). Clearing
that bar across nine blocs this different — Bharat, Zhongguo, the
Eurasian Union, the OIC bloc's own internal Iran-Saudi tension, the
Atlantic Zone's own defensiveness — is genuinely hard, which is why
day-to-day the UN *looks* toothless on Earth even though it isn't
structurally incapable. On the rare occasions consensus does form, it
almost certainly follows OPRA's own real shape (`os-lineages.md`): a
genuine crisis forces real, temporary agreement, the UN exercises
real binding authority while the emergency lasts, and the consensus
dissolves back into normal fragmentation once the fear that produced
it fades, because nobody retains the standing to hold nine blocs
together once it does.

**The UN Human Rights Council is still here, carried forward from
real history rather than reinvented** — the same body (under its real
current name; the real original 1946 Commission, Eleanor Roosevelt's
own, drafted the founding Universal Declaration this corpus's version
descends from unbroken) running the same real periodic-review process
across all nine blocs, housed in the same New York blue-ribbon tier as
everything else ceremonial. It's the broad, standing mandate; the
**UN Committee Against Torture** below is one specific, treaty-bound
committee operating underneath that same umbrella — real UN practice
already works exactly this way, a general Council plus narrower
treaty-specific committees, not one monolithic human-rights office.

**A worked example: the UN Convention Against Torture is one of the
rare cases that actually clears the threshold** — real, already-
existing precedent, since the actual present-day Convention Against
Torture is among the most widely-ratified human-rights treaties on
Earth today despite covering governments with wildly different
politics otherwise. The same logic holds here: no veto power wants to
be the one visible holdout on a ban this close to universal moral
consensus, reputational cost alone clears the bar even where genuine
enforcement never will. So all nine blocs ratify it, and it becomes
real, binding UN law rather than a mere treaty recommendation — proof
the domestic-binding mechanism isn't hypothetical.

**But binding and enforced are different things, and the gap between
them is where the real texture lives.** The UN has no domestic police
power to back the Convention up — its own ground forces stay
treaty-capped (below) regardless of what's binding on paper. A
standing **UN Committee Against Torture**, run out of New York
alongside the other blue-ribbon machinery (above), reviews each
bloc's own self-reported compliance and hears formal complaints — the
same real body the actual Convention already runs, with the same real
limit: no power beyond reporting and reputational pressure. The
International Court (below) can in principle hear an inter-bloc
complaint, but its ruling is only as binding as the accused bloc's own
consent, same as everywhere else on Earth. Actual compliance quality
tracks each bloc's own domestic institutional strength from
`earth-blocs.md`, not the treaty itself: the Atlantic Zone's own
independent judiciary makes violations genuinely harder to hide than
the Zhongguo bloc's or the Eurasian Union's more centralized systems
do — the same real, uneven pattern the actual Convention's signatories
already show today.

**Mars, the Belt, and Doris sit entirely outside it.** The Convention
binds the nine blocs; it binds nobody else, because nobody else is
party to the UN's own caucus system in the first place. MDR sets its
own policy independently, with no obligation to match the UN's
standard either way. Doris — already established as a thin, barely-
staffed, flag-of-convenience jurisdiction (`corporations.md`) — is the
obvious, structural seam: a plausible home for exactly the kind of
activity that can't survive scrutiny inside a ratifying bloc, not
because anyone designed it that way, but because that's precisely what
a flag-of-convenience jurisdiction is for. Haven Corrections Group
(`corporations.md`), the Earth-Mars joint-venture private prison
operator, sits right on this seam too: a real, concrete, unresolved
question about which jurisdiction's compliance regime actually governs
a joint venture's own facilities, and real-world precedent that
private prisons already draw weaker oversight than state-run ones even
inside a single, fully-compliant jurisdiction.

The Sol Industrial Trust (`corporations.md`)
is explicitly the sinister mirror of this same pattern — the one
coalition that formed under crisis and never let go.

**In space, much closer to a real federal government.** Off-world
governance was never any single bloc's to claim, so the UN's
authority here is real and exercised, not theoretical — the Frontier
Opportunity Program, RAP/SPERB conformance certification, its role in
Ganymede's condominium and Europa's Protocol. This is where the UN's
actual power lives.

## The UN and non-bloc populations: the Belt and Jupiter

Neither the Belt nor Jupiter is one of the nine blocs, and the UN's
posture toward each is genuinely different — co-option of an outside
population versus defense of a stake it already holds.

**The Belt gets Permanent Observer status — the same pattern reused
a second time.** A real, existing UN category (used today for
entities like the Holy See): a genuine seat, genuine ambassadors, no
vote on binding resolutions. OSL maintains an actual diplomatic
mission, most plausibly in New Delhi, functioning as its de facto
embassy — the exact same shape as the Belt's own token seat on the
Tripartite Ganymede Commission (`ganymede-condominium.md`), not a
coincidence. **The goal is co-option, not absorption or pure
exploitation.** Full absorption as a tenth voting bloc is a cost the
four veto powers have no reason to pay voluntarily; pure exploitation
with zero recognition risks pushing more of the Belt toward ASA and
destabilizing a resource base the whole system depends on. Observer
status plus the Ceres MFN framework (`ceres.md`) is the cheap, stable
middle path. **The mechanical reason the Belt gets a weaker deal than
Bharat did:** India had concentrated, unified leverage — one bloc,
one funding stream, one tech dependency. The Belt's leverage is real
(the Rockline Compact's suspicions, its informal technical backdoor
at Ganymede, `ganymede-condominium.md`) but never unified, split
between OSL and ASA. That fragmentation is exactly why OSL settles
for an observer seat instead of real power — and exactly the
ammunition ASA already uses against it ("sold out what the Accord
actually meant," `belt-political-structure.md`): OSL settled for
crumbs because it lacked the leverage to demand more.

**Jupiter is the opposite posture: defending an existing seat, not
courting a new population.** The UN isn't trying to bring the Jovian
system into its orbit — it's already a founding party of both the
Tripartite Ganymede Commission and the Europa Allocation Council
(`ganymede-condominium.md`, `europa.md`). Its real goal there is not
getting pushed out by the United Moons' independence push. The
Concord Settlement (`callisto.md`) is exactly this: the UN extending
a formal position it already holds elsewhere in the Jovian system
onto the one piece — Callisto — that isn't shared yet, rather than
risk losing the whole system to full independence.

## Military teeth

**Orbital kinetic capability is a real, existential, and essentially
unusable deterrent.** Controlling orbit means controlling the ability
to drop mass on anything below — real physics, not an invented
weapon. The UN's fleet could theoretically end any member bloc. It
never will, outside total civilizational breakdown, because actually
doing so would horrify and unite every other bloc against the UN
immediately — the same mutually-assured-destruction logic real
nuclear deterrence runs on. The deterrent's entire value is in never
being spent.

**Ground forces are treaty-capped, and deliberately weaker than
Mars's.** By binding agreement — the **Delhi Protocol**, signed
alongside the UN's own relocation of real administrative power to
New Delhi — the UN's standing ground forces are limited to just
enough personnel to crew its own fleet, plus one or two small
expeditionary units. Genuinely insufficient to occupy or coerce any
bloc. This makes the UN structurally weaker in ground capability than
MDR, whose "disciplined, militarized culture" (`mars-political-
structure.md`) gives it proportionally the strongest ground military
in the system — a deliberate asymmetry, not an oversight.

**Orbital embargo is the UN's real, usable lever — not the nuclear
option, the actual one.** Short of kinetic bombardment, control of
orbital trade and transit lanes means the UN fleet can functionally
blockade any bloc: proportionate, reversible, genuinely exercised in
a way destruction never is. Authorizing one requires the same high
consensus threshold as binding domestic policy above — a supermajority
of the blocs' own caucus delegations, not a Secretary-General's
unilateral call.

**Additional Protocol IV (Outer Space) extends the real Geneva
Convention tradition off-world.** The real Geneva Conventions already
have a documented history of exactly this kind of extension — the
1977 and 2005 Additional Protocols broadened the original 1949 text to
new forms of warfare without replacing it — so a further protocol
covering spacefaring conflict is a direct continuation, not an
invented genre of law. Negotiated and signed at Geneva itself, keeping
Earth's own old ceremonial diplomatic tradition as the literal site of
treaty-making even once the subject matter moves off-world, the same
"fossil-naming" logic already running through New York's own standing.
Core provisions: protected, non-targetable status for non-combatant
vessels and anything life-support-critical (habitat domes, water/air
reclamation, medical facilities) — a direct, hard-SF-appropriate
descendant of Geneva's real protection for hospitals; a binding **duty
to rescue**, extending the real 1968 Rescue Agreement's peacetime
courtesy into an active wartime obligation to assist any crew in
genuine distress regardless of affiliation; POW treatment standards
for captured military personnel; and explicit protected/neutral status
for designated infrastructure — OPRA's own relay network
(`belt-political-structure.md`) is named outright as protected under
the Protocol, giving its already-established practical neutrality (both
OSL and ASA have to use it regardless of rivalry) real legal teeth for
the first time.

**This is where the domain split cuts the other way.** On Earth, the
UN Convention Against Torture (above) is binding but functionally
unenforced. Off-world, where the UN's authority is already real and
exercised, Protocol IV violations fall under the International
Court's genuine, enforceable jurisdiction (above) — the same
institution, the same domain split, opposite outcome, because this is
space rather than Earth. **Signatories include parties well outside
the nine blocs**, unlike the Earth-only Torture Convention: Mars
(MDR) signed on as an independent sovereign treaty party via ordinary
diplomacy (`mars-political-structure.md`), and OSL signed on behalf of
the Belt's moderate wing — real mutual self-interest clearing the bar
even among genuine rivals, the same logic that gets real nuclear
powers to ratify arms-control treaties with each other today. **ASA's
own relationship to the Protocol is deliberately left unsettled** —
either never signed or a serial, suspected violator — giving OSL a
concrete legal distinction from ASA beyond rhetoric: one side is a
signatory in good standing, the other isn't, or doesn't act like one.

**This also reframes, rather than replaces, the UN's own ship-class
restraint above.** The UN's deliberate choice not to build a precision
bombardment-capable ship class was already doctrine; Protocol IV gives
it a legal dimension too — a weapon that can't reliably discriminate
between a legitimate target and a Protocol-protected one can't be used
without risking a violation, so avoiding that capability is partly
compliance now, not purely restraint. **Mars's Ballista-class sits on
the other side of that same logic**: its variable-yield, precision-
guided doctrine (`ship-class-codex.md`) is exactly the kind of
weapon capable of Protocol-compliant discrimination, which means MDR's
own operational preference for surgical strikes doubles, if Mars ever
needed it to, as the thing that keeps Ballista-class use defensible
under a treaty Mars actually signed.

**No dedicated bombardment ship class, on purpose.** Full doctrine in
`ship-class-codex.md`: refining the kinetic deterrent into an actual
designed weapon system would mean planning to use it more often and
more precisely, directly undercutting the "unthinkable last resort"
logic the whole deterrent depends on. Mars has no equivalent
hesitation — its own Ballista-class (`mars-political-structure.md`,
`ship-class-codex.md`) is exactly the precision capability the UN
won't build.

**The rogue-command problem has both a political answer and a
technical vulnerability underneath it.** Politically: no single bloc
or individual holds unilateral authorization authority over the
fleet — actually firing on anything or ordering a blockade requires
multi-bloc consensus, the same distributed-control logic as real
nuclear launch requiring multiple independent keys. But that
political safeguard is only as strong as the technical trust-chain
enforcing it. If the fleet's own authorization systems carry any
stale, cached, or long-lived-trust exception anywhere — the same
category of vulnerability already built into Lassell Relay's own
deliberately-approved exception (`lassell-relay.md`) or the cross-
cert/AuthorityWeight problems flagged in `corporations.md` — a
sufficiently positioned insider doesn't need to defeat nine blocs'
worth of political consensus. They need one cached credential nobody's
re-verified recently. The treaty limits exist because everyone
already knows the political safeguard has a technical attack surface
underneath it, not despite knowing it.
