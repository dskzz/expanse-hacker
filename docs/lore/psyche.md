# Psyche: Oberth's Claim

Status: locked, 2026-11-24. Oberth Extraction's home (`corporations.md`)
already anchors here with a LocationChain — this file gives the place
itself the history and character treatment, deliberately built as a
skeleton-crew, heavily-automated extraction site rather than a real
settlement. The low population isn't a gap to fill in later; it's the
point, and the thing worth making interesting.

**LocationChain:** `BELT:PSYCHE:OBERTH`.

## Real facts: a dead world's heart

16 Psyche (~220 km) is the largest metallic (M-type) body in the Belt
— not rock, not ice, overwhelmingly iron-nickel. The leading real
hypothesis is that it's the **exposed core of a shattered
protoplanet**: something that was once differentiated like Vesta, but
lost its entire rocky mantle to one or more violent ancient collisions,
leaving only the metal heart behind. It looks the part — a mottled
grey-silver metallic sheen instead of the dark carbonaceous rubble of
its neighbors, and notably dense for its size. NASA's real Psyche
mission (launched October 2023) exists specifically because this is
the only body humanity can study up close that might be a planet's
core turned inside-out.

**The mythology, free to use because it's real:** popular coverage of
the real mission produced genuine headlines claiming Psyche's metal
content is "worth more than Earth's entire economy" — naive raw-
material math that ignores what flooding the market with that much
nickel-iron would actually do to prices. The math is bad, but the
belief is exactly the kind of thing characters in-universe would
repeat anyway. Oberth doesn't correct it.

## Why hands-off is the business model, not an absence of one

Oberth's actual edge (`corporations.md`) is **exclusive claim rights
over a zone nobody else can economically contest** — not volume, not
innovation, just a moat nobody's allowed to cross. Once that
exclusivity is secured and the Trust's backing (`corporations.md`'s
hidden-ownership section) keeps anyone from seriously challenging it,
the rational move isn't to maximize presence, it's to minimize cost.
Automate everything that can be automated — robotic miners, claim-
transponders, unmanned ore processing and bulk tugs running fixed
routes — and keep the human headcount as close to zero as the
operation can get away with. The site isn't under-invested the way
VPH are; it's deliberately lean, because every dollar not spent on
crew is pure margin on a monopoly nobody can take away.

**A second, colder reason, and the real payoff of the model:** a
population is also a liability. Vesta's decline produced real worker
councils filling a real power vacuum (`asteroid-bases.md`) — Oberth
has clearly learned that lesson without anyone needing to say so out
loud. Keep crews small, keep contracts short, rotate people out before
they put down roots, and there's no community left to organize in the
first place. Psyche isn't hands-off by neglect; it's hands-off *by
design*, specifically so nothing like Vesta's councils can ever form
here.

## Who's actually there

A handful of contract personnel, rotated on short tours, doing the
jobs automation genuinely can't: hardware the robots can't self-
diagnose, physical maintenance, certification renewals, claim-boundary
defense, and liaison back to Oberth corporate. Some of the operation's
oversight likely isn't even performed on-site — plausibly run
remotely from a nearby hub (Ceres is the obvious candidate given its
UN/MFN administrative weight, `ceres.md`) with only safety-critical
or hands-on maintenance work actually requiring a body physically on
the rock. Nobody on a Psyche contract thinks of it as home. That's
intentional, and it shows: there's no settlement name worth
remembering here, no bare-name-earned-by-character the way Vesta or
Davida got one — just "Oberth's claim," cold and bureaucratic, because
nothing about the place has ever been personal enough to earn
anything warmer.

## What this actually exposes

A site that runs mostly on automation with minimal human oversight is
exactly the kind of long-unaudited system `os-lineages.md`'s drift
thesis is built on — years of robotic claim-transponders and
processing logic running on cached authorization nobody's re-checked,
because there's barely anyone there whose job it would even be to
check. A few concrete angles that follow directly from the model,
not bolted on:

- **The claim-provenance fraud hook already flagged in
  `corporations.md`** ("who actually has the real claim here") has an
  obvious home: with almost no human witnesses on-site, automated
  claim records and extraction logs effectively *are* the only record
  of what happened — and a log with no one around to contradict it is
  a log that's trivially worth forging.
- **Robots don't know a claim is being disputed.** If someone
  physically contests Oberth's exclusivity on the ground — another
  Belt operator, a Rockline Compact member testing the moat, ASA
  making a point — the automated systems just keep extracting per
  their last valid cached authorization, because nothing on-site is
  positioned to notice a dispute is even happening. The enforcement
  of "nobody else can economically contest this" turns out to rest
  almost entirely on paperwork and Trust backing, not presence —
  which is a real vulnerability the moment either one gets
  successfully challenged.
- **A rallying example, not a recruiting ground.** Psyche has no
  organic Belt political foothold the way VPH do — nobody lives here
  long enough to radicalize. But that absence is itself useful to
  ASA: Psyche is the clean, finished version of what hands-off Trust-
  backed capital wants for the whole Belt — all the extraction, none
  of the people, nothing left to organize against because nothing's
  left to organize *at all*. ASA doesn't need Psyche's crew to make
  that argument; the empty rock makes it for them.
