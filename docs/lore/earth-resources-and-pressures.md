# Earth: Resource Pressures and Allocation

Status: new file, 2026-11-25. Earth is the corpus's central frame, not
one location among many — it needs real structural load-bearing, "double
studs," in the user's own words, that a single moon or station doesn't.
This file is that extra bracing: real demographic and resource pressure
per bloc (not invented crises, extrapolations of trends already visible
today), and the basic shape of who feeds whom and who builds what.
Companion to `earth-blocs.md` (internal governance) and
`earth-political-structure.md` (the UN layer above all nine).

## Demographic and social pressure, bloc by bloc

Different blocs face genuinely different pressures, not a single
"overpopulated dystopian Earth" template — some are shrinking, some are
straining under growth, and the mismatch between the two is itself a
live source of friction.

**The Atlantic Zone** — real, already-visible demographic decline
carried forward: a shrinking, aging population, with its aggregate
wealth (old money, the Trust, Hauptmann & Voskuijlen) concentrated
rather than broadly shared. The result is a real two-tier society —
the bloc stays wealthy on paper while its older industrial and
post-industrial cities see genuine urban decay and crime, the wealth
visibly not reaching the people living in them. That mismatch is the
Zone's own domestic version of the exact same formal-legitimacy-vs-
real-power throughline running through everything else in this corpus.
Domestically, it produces a real, recognizable political cleavage
between a globalist, old-money-aligned establishment and a nativist,
decline-focused opposition — the same split real Atlantic-world
politics already shows signs of today.

**The African Union** — the opposite pressure: real continued
population growth, a genuine youth bulge straining food, housing, and
infrastructure even as it's also the bloc's real long-term asset (a
large, young workforce other blocs increasingly don't have). Managing
that double edge — growth as both strain and opportunity — is the
AU's own central domestic debate, and it's exactly why the bloc is the
Proportionality Caucus's clearest remaining example: real, growing
material weight, no veto seat to show for it.

**Bharat** — still growing, still young, and its defining pressure is
feeding that population: a domestic agricultural base that can't
scale cleanly with demand at this size, the real tension already
visible in India's own present-day food-security debates. There's a
real irony worth keeping: Bharat secured a UN veto seat through
funding and tech leverage, yet a basic material need — reliably
feeding itself — remains a genuine vulnerability underneath that
formal power, the same "legitimacy without full control" shape as
MDR's own Elysium question. Domestically, agricultural policy is
accordingly a live, central axis of party competition, not a side
issue.

**The Zhongguo-descended bloc** — a double pressure, worse than either
the Atlantic Zone's or Bharat's single one: a real, already-documented
aging/declining birth-rate crisis, layered on top of limited arable
land relative to population, meaning real dependency on imported food
despite centuries of domestic agricultural investment. That's a
genuine structural vulnerability sitting underneath a proud, centralized
political culture that resents sharing status (`flashpoints.md`'s
Bharat-veto resentment) — the bloc most publicly insistent on its own
strength is also, materially, one of the more exposed.

**The Eurasian Union** — Russia's own real demographic decline, similar
in shape to the Atlantic Zone's but without a comparable wealth
cushion, leaning instead on resource extraction (oil, gas) for real
leverage. That fossil-fuel dependency gives it a genuine, if unspoken,
shared interest with the OIC bloc (below) in not seeing the fusion
economy displace fossil leverage too quickly — an unlikely alignment
between two blocs that otherwise share very little.

**Patria Grande** — real, stark wealth disparity and urban crime
concentrated in its megacities, the same recognizable pattern as actual
present-day Latin America, but offset by genuine material strength as
a resource and food exporter (below). That's the same shape of
leverage-without-formal-power Bharat held before its veto seat — worth
keeping as a live, undecided question about whether Patria Grande ever
presses that leverage the way Bharat did.

**The Three Seas-descended bloc** — a double demographic squeeze
distinct from the Atlantic Zone's: real emigration toward the
wealthier Atlantic Zone hollowing out the workforce, on top of its own
low birth rates. That ongoing drain is the material reason this bloc
stays structurally dependent on the Atlantic Zone despite nominal
independence — not just the cultural affinity already flagged in
`earth-political-structure.md`, a real demographic one too.

**ASEAN and Others** — the strain of rapid industrialization rather
than population decline: real environmental and resource cost from
serving as much of Earth's manufacturing floor (below), the believable
extrapolation of today's actual Mekong-basin and deforestation
pressures. Its pet issue is managing that cost without losing the
manufacturing role that's also its real economic strength.

**The OIC-descended bloc** — the bloc fusion power left behind. Real
Gulf-state oil wealth and Iran's own hydrocarbon economy were the
foundation of this bloc's leverage for generations; a maturing fusion
economy — deuterium and helium-3, not fossil fuel — erodes exactly that
foundation, the same real anxiety already driving actual present-day
OPEC behavior around the renewable-energy transition, just carried
further. That gives this bloc a direct, material reason to resist
anything that accelerates the fusion transition — not environmental or
safety objections, naked economic self-interest. See "The OIC bloc's
fusion-era anxiety" below for where this actually bites.

## Who feeds whom

Not a flat "Earth imports from space" story — real exporters and real
importers, with the gap itself a source of leverage:

- **Net exporters:** Patria Grande (the real global breadbasket —
  grain, soy, beef, extrapolated directly from Brazil's and
  Argentina's actual current agricultural dominance), the Eurasian
  Union (Russia's real wheat-export strength), the Atlantic Zone's own
  North American agricultural base (which stays a real exporter even
  as the rest of the bloc's demographic picture declines), and ASEAN
  and Others (continuing Southeast Asia's real rice-export tradition).
- **Net importers:** Bharat and the Zhongguo-descended bloc (both
  despite large domestic agriculture — scale of population outstrips
  it at this size, the same real gap already visible today), and the
  OIC bloc (arid climate, a real, long-standing import dependency for
  basic foodstuffs). The African Union sits in between — real gains
  made since today, not full self-sufficiency.

This matters for the same reason Bharat's veto seat does: Patria
Grande, despite holding no veto, has real, checkable material leverage
over two veto powers' own food security. Whether it ever presses that
leverage the way Bharat pressed its own funding/tech leverage is an
open question, not a settled one.

## Who makes things

A rough three-tier map, extrapolated from real, already-visible
present-day trends rather than invented from nothing:

- **The Atlantic Zone** holds real, continued dominance in advanced
  and aerospace-grade manufacturing — the high end of the value chain,
  including most of what actually goes into military and deep-space
  hardware. This is the material fact underneath the Atlantic Zone's
  contracting leverage below.
- **The Zhongguo-descended bloc** has moved up-market from low-cost
  mass manufacturing into high-value industrial and technology
  production — the real, already-underway "moving up the value chain"
  trend, just further along.
- **ASEAN and Others and Bharat** compete for the low-to-mid-cost mass
  manufacturing role China once held exclusively — the real present-day
  "China+1" diversification trend, carried to its logical endpoint:
  two real competitors for the same role rather than one successor.

## The OIC bloc's fusion-era anxiety

This is where the bloc's economic displacement (above) becomes active
politics rather than background texture, and it deepens two threads
already running rather than adding an isolated new one.

**At the Europa Allocation Council** (`europa.md`,
`flashpoints.md`), Frostline Harvesting's OPEC-style cartel backing of
the UN's conservative harvesting-quota position already has a quiet
money motive. The OIC bloc gives the UN's own conservative stance a
second, independent, mutually-reinforcing cause: real political
pressure from inside the UN's own caucus, from a bloc with every
reason to see deuterium harvesting stay slow — not cartel money this
time, bloc self-interest. Two separate actors wanting the same
outcome for different reasons is more realistic than one hidden hand,
not less.

**At Thisbe Station** (`thisbe-station.md`), the same motive extends
further: any serious move to expand helium-3 extraction from the
discovery that grew out of the Massacre's aftermath is something the
OIC bloc has a direct material reason to slow or oppose at the UN
level, regardless of the cooperative's own internal politics about
what to do with the discovery. The bloc holds no veto, so it can't
block this outright — but it can lobby hard within the UN's own
caucus and Committee of the Whole, the same channel already
established for ordinary UN-level influence
(`earth-political-structure.md`), making this a real, standing
pressure on any future UN decision about Thisbe's discovery rather
than a one-time objection.

## Waste as an export, not a burial problem

A standing, real-world-grounded pressure none of the above quite
covers: a crowded, resource-strained Earth genuinely needs somewhere
to put what it can't recycle or bury safely. The popular image of
firing it straight into the Sun doesn't survive real orbital
mechanics — canceling Earth's own 29.8 km/s orbital velocity costs
more delta-v than escaping the solar system outright, making the Sun
one of the single most expensive destinations anywhere in it. The
actual answer is cheaper and more useful: a mass-driver launch complex
sited on **Chimborazo**, Ecuador — a real, specific, deliberately-
chosen site, not an arbitrary one. Because of Earth's own equatorial
bulge, Chimborazo's summit is the single point on Earth's surface
farthest from Earth's center (farther than Everest, despite being
shorter in raw elevation), and it sits almost exactly on the equator,
giving it both the thinnest practical atmosphere to punch through and
the planet's maximum free rotational velocity boost (~465 m/s
eastward) for anything launched with the spin rather than against it.
Real, if speculative, engineering lineage — electromagnetic launch-
assist concepts (real studies like "StarTram") have proposed exactly
this combination for exactly this reason.

**Officially the Equatorial Bulk Export Program, a joint UN/Patria
Grande venture** — Ecuador provides the geography, the UN provides the
system-wide waste-management mandate — compacted, inert waste gets
launched outbound on a cheap trajectory toward the Belt, where it's
genuinely useful raw bulk material rather than garbage at all: direct
feedstock for Driftwood Salvage & Reclamation
(`corporations.md`) and the broader scrapshell salvage economy that
already runs on hand-me-down and reclaimed material. Not a disposal
scheme dressed up as recycling — a real, if grim, economic chain, the
same "formal legitimacy vs. real power" throughline this corpus runs
on everywhere else: officially a bulk-materials program, popularly
mocked as **the Trash Train**.

**Receiving, sorting, and reclaiming it is real, grim, low-status
labor** — a genuine Belt-side waste-picker economy, with a direct
real-world precedent: several real Indian cities run substantial
informal waste-picking economies today, and that same hard-won
expertise plausibly travels outward with India's own already-
established diaspora (`frontier-timeline.md`), making the Belt's own
garbage pickers a continuation of a real labor tradition rather than
an invented one. Gives Ecuador/Patria Grande a genuine flagship piece
of orbital infrastructure to be proud of, the same way other blocs
already have one, while the actual dirty, dangerous end of the chain
lands, as it usually does in this corpus, on whoever has the least
leverage to refuse it.
