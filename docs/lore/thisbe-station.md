# Thisbe Station

Status: locked, 2026-11-24. Seventh full station write-up, and the
corpus's actual founding-trauma site — the Expanse-decoupled
replacement for "Anderson Station" (`os-lineages.md` §1, §7). Settled
on the deliberate "uneasy third way": neither fully redeemed nor a
dead memorial.

**LocationChain:** `BELT:THISBE` — sits directly on the real 88
Thisbe asteroid, no intermediate region needed, added as part of the
corpus-wide LocationChain retrofit pass.

## The real rock, best understanding

**88 Thisbe** — a real, fairly large main-belt asteroid, roughly 200+
km across, among the couple dozen biggest bodies in the belt.
Discovered in 1866. Classified as a carbonaceous-type body (possibly
specifically B-type, the same family as Bennu, the real OSIRIS-REx
target) — confident on size and general classification, less so on
fine orbital specifics, which should be treated loosely. Carbonaceous
asteroids are exactly the type richest in volatiles: water ice,
hydrated minerals, organic compounds — the same resource category
Frostline Harvesting's whole business runs on, and the same stuff
feeding deuterium extraction for fusion fuel. Sits at an ordinary,
accessible position within the main belt, on normal shipping lanes —
not an exotic outer-system location, not a special pilgrimage site.
People pass through constantly without meaning to.

## The massacre and the reform window

~2290: a surrender message gets flagged low-priority, drifts through
Earth's civilian filters, and is silently dropped. Elias Voss orders
an assault based on silence the network itself manufactured — the
founding trauma of this entire setting. The administrative authority
running Thisbe at the time was Earth/UN-adjacent, consistent with the
message being filtered by "Earth's civilian filters" in the first
place.

The **Ceres Broadcast** (~2290s–2299) made the massacre public
knowledge system-wide, and the old authority's credibility didn't
survive the scandal. In the resulting vacuum — nobody from the UN,
Mars, or a Belt org stepped in to replace it — Thisbe's surviving
workforce organized into a genuine **workers' cooperative**, self-
governed rather than imposed. This is plausibly the earliest real-
world proof of concept behind **the New International**
(`earth-political-structure.md`) — not pure theory, actual history.

## Strangled from outside, not corrupted from within

Deliberately not an Animal Farm story — the co-op didn't betray its
own ideals from the inside. It worked, genuinely, for a real stretch.
What actually wore it down was the same mechanism running elsewhere
in this corpus: inner-system finance capital (Hauptmann & Voskuijlen-
style debt leverage, market undercutting from Trust-adjacent volatile/
ice interests) picking at a resource-rich but politically inconvenient
cooperative until it couldn't compete on its own terms. Structural
defeat, not moral failure — capital quietly winning because the rules
favor capital, the same pattern running through Ceres's MFN framework
and the Rockline Compact's whole grievance.

## Space Cuba: the actual present-day shape

Still a cooperative, in name and mostly in practice — hollowed out,
not thriving, not dead. The real analogy worth using directly, and
more precisely than "everyone avoids it": most of the world doesn't
actually avoid Cuba — plenty of nations maintain completely normal
relations with it specifically to signal independence from US
pressure, or just to needle Washington a little. The accurate version
here is an active three-way split, not uniform hands-off avoidance:

- **The UN** can't move against Thisbe without reopening the
  massacre's own wound publicly — the same broadcast that created the
  reform window in the first place would make any crackdown now a
  system-wide scandal. Genuinely stuck, not merely reluctant.
- **OSL has been the real, decades-long quiet patron** — not reserved
  at all, just patient. Overtly backing Thisbe would hand the UN
  exactly the "Belt political aggression" pretext it needs, so OSL
  played the long game instead: quietly keeping the cooperative fed,
  healthy, and newsworthy for years, understanding its value as a
  sympathetic cause without ever claiming credit or provoking the UN
  directly. Careful and genuinely invested at the same time, not one
  instead of the other — the patient version of OSL's established
  diplomatic caution, not an exception to it.
- **ASA** doesn't need to actually embrace the cooperative's ideology
  to use open relations with Thisbe as a deliberate wedge against the
  UN — opportunism dressed as solidarity, consistent with ASA's
  established willingness to act unilaterally and provoke rather than
  negotiate.
- **MDR** maintains a small, calculated, visibly-public trade
  relationship for the same reason, scaled to Mars's own insecurity
  about its unequal relationship with Earth (`mariner-valley.md`) —
  enough open contact to be a pointed irritant, not enough exposure to
  invite real cost.

The UN hates all of it and can't do much about any single piece:
can't crack down on Thisbe without the scandal, can't credibly punish
Mars or ASA for maintaining ordinary relations with a nominally-
still-recognized cooperative without looking like a bully on top of
everything else. Thisbe can't fully break free either — it still
needs inner-system markets and capital it has no real way to replace.
A real, active diplomatic pressure point, not a forgotten backwater
everyone quietly avoids.

## The big carrot: a tightrope OSL now has to actually walk

Decades of quiet investment suddenly pays off: the helium-3 discovery
gives OSL something real enough to consider openly laying claim to
mineral rights on Thisbe's behalf, for the first time risking an
actual, open breach with Earth rather than quiet patronage. The
parallel is deliberate and worth stating directly: Mars secured fuel
independence via Phobos and Deimos (`mars-political-structure.md`);
this is the Belt's own shot at the same thing. If it works, Earth
becomes the comparatively exposed power — the only one left still
dependent on a strained, politically fraught supply (Luna's depleting
ice, the UN's own administrative mess, `armstrong-city.md`) once both
Mars and the Belt have independent sources. A real, system-wide
strategic shift riding on one decision, not a local mineral-rights
dispute.

Genuinely unresolved: whether the opportunity for the Belt to have
its own fuel source is worth the open breach with Earth, or whether
OSL's whole decades-long investment gets risked and lost on one bad
roll. Not decided here on purpose.

## Mars called in to mediate, and a fault line that was already there

The stakes are high enough that **Mars gets called in to mediate**
between OSL and the UN — plausible specifically because Mars is
independent enough not to be Earth's rubber stamp, but not as
confrontational as the Belt's hardline factions, giving it real
credibility as a broker neither side fully trusts but both can at
least talk to.

Mediating surfaces a fault line already built into MDR rather than
creating a new one: **the Commonwealth of Elysium sides with the
Belt's claim**, consistent with its own collectivist political
character and its general sympathy toward exactly this kind of
worker-cooperative cause. **The Mariner Valley Caucus**
(`mars-political-structure.md`) sides with the UN instead — the first
real, live issue to actually activate the Consular College's
13th-seat tension and the Congress's own internal split, rather than
leaving it as background texture. Mars doesn't mediate as one unified
voice; it mediates while quietly split against itself.

**Chronic underemployment, and — the part that actually matters for
play — chronically underpatched software.** Nobody with real capital
wants to invest in a place with this history and this politics, so
the cooperative is permanently cash-strapped. A station that can't
afford proper IT maintenance is a sympathetic, believable reason for
genuinely vulnerable network infrastructure — the same shape as
Shackleton's neglect (`armstrong-city.md`), different root cause:
underinvestment born of squeeze, not abandonment born of irrelevance.

## The helium-3 discovery: recent, regional, and a live complication

**Explicitly recent** — within the last few years of "now," not old
settled history. This is a contemporary issue the present political
situation hasn't finished reacting to, not backstory.

**Regional, not a single freak rock** — a better, more scientifically
coherent conceit than one isolated pocket at Thisbe alone. Real
asteroid "families" exist: groups of bodies sharing a common
collisional origin and similar composition, the same way the real
Flora, Eos, and Themis families do. If Thisbe's whole neighborhood
shares common ancestry — a parent body that broke apart at some
point, or a cluster of bodies with similar impact-exposure
histories — then the entire region being helium-3-rich is more
plausible than a single lucky exception, and it raises the stakes
considerably: not one valuable station, a whole valuable neighborhood
suddenly worth fighting over.

Within that region, Thisbe specifically holds an unusually rich,
localized pocket of helium-3-bearing regolith — plausibly exposed by
some specific impact event that uncovered ancient, long-undisturbed
surface material, rather
than a uniform planet-wide deposit. Solar-wind-implanted helium-3
isn't exclusively lunar; it accumulates on any long-exposed airless
surface, just at a lower rate farther from the Sun — a genuinely
unusual, surprising find for a Belt body, not a rule-break.

This threatens the equilibrium above right when the squeeze was
working: the UN wanted Thisbe weak and dependent, and a real windfall
undermines that. **Genuinely open, not resolved:** does external
pressure to seize or control Thisbe intensify now that the stakes are
higher, or does the temptation of real wealth to manage pull at the
cooperative's own leadership — the exact corruption it was built to
avoid, approached from a different direction than Animal Farm's
internal betrayal, but a live risk all the same?

## The cooperative isn't one voice on the discovery — a real three-way split

Resolving part of the open question above, not the whole thing: the
helium-3 find doesn't land on a unified leadership with one plan, it
lands on a cooperative that disagrees with itself, a real internal
opposition dynamic rather than Thisbe speaking as one voice for the
first time in this corpus.

- **A develop-carefully faction** wants to work the discovery slowly,
  on the cooperative's own terms, specifically to build the kind of
  real leverage Mars built with Phobos and Deimos (above) — autonomy
  through fuel independence, not a quick payout.
- **A keep-it-hidden faction** wants the opposite: the discovery kept
  as quiet as possible, specifically to avoid drawing exactly the
  outside attention already built into this corpus — the UN Nuclear
  Safeguards Directorate's own fuel-cycle inspection regime and the
  OIC bloc's fusion-era anxiety (`space-law-compacts.md`,
  `earth-resources-and-pressures.md`) both have a real, concrete
  reason to come looking the moment Thisbe's helium-3 becomes
  official rather than rumored.
- **A cash-out faction** wants to sell the leverage for maximum
  immediate material gain, politics aside — the cooperative's
  chronic underinvestment and underemployment (above) make this
  faction's appeal genuinely sympathetic, not cynical for its own
  sake.

None of the three has named leadership yet, and none has won — a live
split, not a resolved internal debate, sitting underneath the
already-established three-way external pressure (UN/OSL/ASA/MDR)
above it.
