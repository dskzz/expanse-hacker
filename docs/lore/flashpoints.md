# Flashpoints

Status: tracking doc, created 2026-11-24, swept for a full first audit
2026-11-24. Where `people-pass-queue.md` tracks every unnamed figure
awaiting a name, this file tracks every live, unresolved tension
across the corpus loaded enough to actually erupt into something —
not just an undecided detail, a standing fault line. Add to this list
as new ones come up or get identified; an entry graduates off this
list only when it's actually resolved in its home file, not when it's
merely been written about.

## Jupiter system

### The Jovian sovereignty crisis

Home file: `callisto.md`. The Callisto Home Rule League's push for
self-governance, its maximalist United Moons splinter's pan-Jovian
independence ambition, and the UN/MDR/OSL's own negotiated Concord
Settlement (a real partition, not an invented complication) are all
live at once, with three mutually exclusive endings on the table and
nothing deciding which wins. The single largest unresolved political
thread in the corpus right now.

### Ganymede's native water drilling project, and who's quietly against it

Home file: `ganymede-condominium.md`. A real drilling project to tap
Ganymede's own deep water table is finally starting, after decades of
being stalled by the legitimate-sounding objection that Europa's own
protected ocean (`europa.md`) should already be precedent enough — but
some of that opposition traces back to a quiet Saturnite influence
campaign, plausibly run through House Dalreoch's own outside contacts
(`saturn.md`), protecting Saturn's own water-export revenue from a
success that would undercut Jupiter's dependency on it entirely. A
rare point where Saturn's own rival Highland and Lowland sides share
real interest rather than feuding. No outcome decided — whether the
drilling succeeds, and what happens to Jupiter/Saturn's own mutual
dependency if it does, is deliberately open.

### Jupiter and Saturn's own cold war, with a real twenty-year rhythm

Home file: `ganymede-condominium.md`, `saturn.md`. Real interdependency
(Jupiter needs Saturn's water, Saturn needs Jupiter's market access
and manufactured goods) keeps this a cold war rather than a hot one,
the same structural shape as a real rival-but-interdependent regional
power pair — but the real ~19.86-year Jupiter/Saturn synodic period
(the actual astronomical basis for the real "Great Conjunction") gives
it an actual tempo: long quiet stretches, then windows roughly every
twenty years where cheap transit makes mutual raiding and poaching
worth the risk, a real historical Border Reivers dynamic rather than
one-sided villainy on either side. The native-water fight above is a
proxy for this deeper anxiety, not a separate dispute.

### The Ganymede Condominium's three-way tensions

Home file: `ganymede-condominium.md`. Distinct from the sovereignty
crisis above, even though both are Jovian: this is the live friction
*inside* the already-working TGC arrangement, not a fight over
whether it survives. The Belt wants real input on joint defense
decisions and gets only a token seat; Earth and Mars jockey against
each other for greater relative control of that same defense
arrangement; Mars keeps muscling into Ganymede's research despite
its own stated preference for keeping scientists local, because the
agricultural science is real, licensable money. Underneath all of
it, the Belt holds informal technical leverage over the orbital
infrastructure that the other two may not even know about.

### The Europa Allocation Council's quota fight

Home file: `europa.md`. A resource-and-science fight, not a
governance one: the UN, MDR, and OSL each push genuinely different
agendas on harvesting quotas (conservation, fuel security, Belt
equity), no bloc can carry a vote alone, and Frostline Harvesting's
quiet, OPEC-style cartel backing of the UN's conservative position
means the "science versus industry" framing hides real, self-
interested money on both sides. A deadlock just freezes the current
quota — inertia, not crisis, until someone builds a real majority. The
OIC bloc's own fusion-era anxiety (below) now gives the UN's
conservative position a second, independent motive sitting right next
to Frostline's cartel money, not replacing it.

## Mars

### Elysium's devolution friction with MDR

Home file: `mars-political-structure.md`. A standing, textured
political fact rather than a subplot about rebellion: real autonomy
referendums, real disputes over how far the Commonwealth's own
assembly can override Mariner Valley doctrine, real resentment on
both sides that's never quite boiled into actual separatism — yet.

### The Martian Congress's malapportionment crisis

Home file: `mariner-valley.md`. The Congress's original twelve
colony-seats are increasingly hollow — old, shrinking, symbolically
important but materially diminishing — while real population,
economy, and capability concentrate elsewhere, Elysium's own 13th
seat above all. The same real structural tension behind historical
"rotten borough" reform crises: formal representation and actual
power have quietly stopped matching, and nobody with the standing to
fix it has any incentive to.

### The Mariner Valley Caucus vs. Elysium bloc rivalry

Home file: `mars-political-structure.md`. Distinct from the
devolution friction above: this is the live voting rivalry between
two organized blocs inside MDR's own institutions, not a fight over
Elysium's autonomy itself. Already activated once, not theoretical —
the Caucus's first real use was siding with the UN against Elysium-
backed Belt interests in the Thisbe helium-3 dispute
(`thisbe-station.md`) — and the underlying rivalry didn't go away
once that particular fight ended.

## The Belt

### The OSL/ASA split

Home file: `belt-political-structure.md`. The Belt's foundational
fault line, which every other Belt flashpoint below is a specific,
concrete instance of: two organizations both claiming to be the
legitimate heir of the same founding Anti-Solar Accord, disagreeing
about what it actually requires — OSL diplomatic and recognition-
seeking, ASA defiant and increasingly paramilitary-coded.

### Vesta's worker councils vs. absentee ownership

Home file: `asteroid-bases.md`. Not background texture — a real,
ongoing contest over who actually runs the place: on paper, a
skeleton administrative presence representing a distant Trust-
affiliated portfolio holder; in practice, ASA-aligned worker
councils holding real day-to-day legitimacy on the ground. The
Belt's angriest, most organized ASA cell, for well-earned economic
reasons rather than assigned ones.

### Ceres: Oduya versus Piast

Home file: `ceres.md`. Hetman Sulayman Piast, a Belter owned lock,
stock, and barrel by Earth, carrot-and-stick, running rigged-but-real
elections — against Imara Oduya's standing opposition. Live, ongoing,
and the Belt's most visible single political rivalry.

### Ceres's gravity fight: a problem that was already solved, broken on purpose

Home file: `ceres.md`. The sharpest, most physical instance yet of
Oduya versus Piast, not a separate dispute — and the real joke
underneath it is that Cole Spindle Works' engineers had already solved
it: at the original 0.5g operating point, the lower-class tier's own
gravity already sits close to a third of a g, quietly, as a side
effect of the geometry, with nobody having had to fight for it. Piast
— genuinely multi-generational Ceres stock himself, not an outsider —
breaks that working compromise anyway by decreeing a single module
(reckless, not the physics-sound paired version, leaving a real
unbalanced structural load on the surrounding rock nobody's addressed)
spun up to its full 0.75g design capacity, publicly framed as
modernization, actually quiet training wheels for his own body ahead
of the comfortable off-Belt retirement he's already after, at the cost
of housing stock for Earth immigrants who haven't even arrived yet. He
defends it by pointing out that
the poor already live at roughly a third of a g in his own upgraded
building — a quiet assertion that low gravity is simply the natural
condition for the poor, not a real defense. A literal physical barrier
results: residents adapted to lower gravity for four to six
generations can't simply move into the upgraded module, meaning a
Belter family could genuinely split across the two gravity regimes and
never visit each other again without real health risk. The movement's
own chant, "Slow down the drums! Point Three G!", is catchy precisely
because it isn't the real policy — taken literally it would push the
worst-off residents down toward a genuinely debilitating ~0.13–0.2g,
and actually fixing it without that own-goal would mean tearing down
and rebuilding the premium tier's own ceilings, an unrealistic
retrofit. Oduya's real platform underneath the chant is smarter: let
Piast keep his Ugly Spin, and build a brand-new, purpose-built 0.3g
module as its counter-rotating partner — cheaper than Piast's own
project and the real fix for the structural vulnerability he left
dangling, solving two problems with one build. That real compromise
exists as an internal white paper, dry and bureaucratic on its face —
exactly what makes it so damaging once a hired consultant (client
deliberately unclear; ASA's own hardline wing, already on record
considering her approach "too compromised," is the obvious candidate)
has it stolen and leaked stripped of context, running the next day as
"Oduya Sells Out to Piast" and "Oduya Backs Down on Piast's Ugly
Spin." Live, unresolved, and already Ceres's own flashpoint subset,
not a hypothetical.

### Ceres's Margins: unmapped rock, unlicensed trade

Home file: `ceres.md`. The ~700m–1km of stationary rock left standing
between every pair of modules is honest engineering, a real margin of
structural safety — but its own real size plausibly hides older,
unofficial bores nobody's ever filed paperwork for, running straight
into Downtown's own accepted-but-unlicensed vice trade at the Twin
Cans' shoddy, lightly-regulated shaft-and-hub docks. Nobody in
authority has mapped it fully, Palisade included, which is exactly
what makes it useful to whoever already knows it — and exactly the
kind of half-mapped space a hacker could get pulled into either
charting or protecting, for very different clients.

### Psyche's live ASA asset among the holdouts

Home file: `psyche.md`. A standing intelligence exposure, not a
resolved fact: the primary ASA asset is specifically the single
irreplaceable systems holdout Oberth can't afford to audit closely,
and a local admin's unheeded suspicion about the resulting leak is
still sitting unresolved at the exact corporate level with no
appetite to investigate it.

### The Massacre at Thisbe Station's unfinished business

Home file: `thisbe-station.md`. The founding trauma itself is settled
history, but its consequences aren't: the live helium-3 crisis
(regional scarcity colliding with the cooperative's own survival) and
the open question of who currently leads the cooperative and what
they actually decide to do with the discovery remain unresolved,
decades after the massacre itself.

## Saturn

### Saturn's atmospheric skimming operation as a Clearances-style displacement

Home file: `saturn.md`. The one Ringer occupation the clans don't
really control: skimming Saturn's upper atmosphere for hydrogen and,
more valuably, helium-3 demands capital and engineering depth no
single clan fields alone, so it runs as a UN/MDR-licensed consortium
operating over the Ringers' own nominal claims — a real, uncomfortable
echo of the actual Highland Clearances, where absentee landlords
displaced small tenant holdings in favor of larger, more profitable
operations dressed in the language of efficiency and modernization.
No clan has a real seat in how it's run or who profits. Structural for
now, not yet a named incident. Now sharper still, with a named face:
House Dalreoch, based at Rhea itself and enmeshed day-to-day with
Cassini Works, already brokers much of the outside trade in processed
ring ice and skimmed fuel — the consortium's natural, profiting local
partner, and a live, specific grievance for Highland clans (Dunmore,
Ardcarrick, the Stanitsa), not an abstract principle.

### The Highland/Lowland split over chartering

Home file: `saturn.md`. Distinct from clan-on-clan rivalry: a regional,
cultural fault line cutting across it, the real Highland/Lowland
divide ported directly rather than invented fresh. Mercat's own two
leading houses don't even agree on tactics — House Fennick wants a
Jovian Development Company-style charter (`callisto.md`) earned slowly,
House Kerrow wants it grabbed outright — but both keep pushing the
Gathering toward becoming something closer to a standing government to
get there. The three Highland clans see both moves as trading real
clan sovereignty for outside legitimacy, and Ardcarrick in particular
has real leverage to make that resistance stick: it holds the single
busiest plow-ship lane Lowland trade actually depends on. Neither side
has won. Whether the charter is ever actually granted, and what it
would cost the Gathering if it is, stays deliberately open.

### Saturn as the system's second practical helium-3 source

Home file: `saturn.md`. Distinct from the tension above: this is about
what Saturn's atmospheric operation *means* for everyone else, not who
controls it locally. Ganymede's own long-term research into mining
Jupiter's atmosphere stays deliberately impractical
(`ganymede-condominium.md`) because Jupiter's gravity well is too
deep — Saturn's shallower well is the one real difference that tips
the same idea from research dream to marginally viable operation now,
making it the second genuinely practical helium-3/fusion-fuel source
in the system after Luna's own reserves, right as those reserves are
plausibly running thin (Lunar resource depletion, below). That's real
strategic weight landing on a place with no standing government and no
unified voice to negotiate with — exactly the condition every outside
power courting an individual Ringer clan is already positioned to
exploit.

## Cross-cutting / Earth

### The OIC bloc's fusion-era anxiety

Home file: `earth-resources-and-pressures.md`. A maturing deuterium/
helium-3 fusion economy erodes the fossil-fuel leverage the OIC bloc's
real wealth has run on for generations — the same anxiety already
driving actual present-day OPEC behavior around the renewable
transition, just carried further. That gives the bloc a direct,
material (not environmental or safety) motive to slow the fusion
transition wherever it can reach it politically: a second, independent
cause behind the UN's own conservative Europa Allocation Council
position (`europa.md`), sitting right alongside Frostline Harvesting's
cartel money rather than replacing it, and a standing, not yet acted
on, source of UN-level pressure against any serious expansion of
Thisbe Station's own helium-3 discovery (`thisbe-station.md`).

### Zhongguo's resentment over Bharat's veto seat

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. Bharat's admission as a
fourth permanent UN veto power came from real leverage (funding, tech
dependency), not fairness, and the Zhongguo-descended bloc was the
last, most reluctant holdout — real India-China regional rivalry
means it experienced the admission as a direct, personal demotion in
exactly the arena it cares about most, in a way the Atlantic Zone and
Eurasian Union never felt as sharply. The resentment never actually
went away; it's live, standing friction inside the same four-power
club that's supposed to present a united front protecting its own
privileges.

### The Proportionality Caucus versus old money's funding leverage

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. The nine blocs' real
demographic and economic weight has shifted hard toward Bharat,
Zhongguo, and the African Union — but the Atlantic Zone's own share
of real wealth hasn't shrunk nearly as fast, and UN funding runs on
assessed contributions proportional to economic weight, not
population. That gives old money a real, checkable lever
(disproportionate funding share buying influence no formal vote
grants) against the loyal Proportionality Caucus's entirely
reasonable, entirely stalled push to reapportion representation to
match the real shift. Distinct from the New International below: this
is reform-from-within friction, not revolutionary rupture.

### The UN Fleet's rogue-command vulnerability

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. The political safeguard
against a rogue Secretary-General or Fleet Commander — multi-bloc
consensus required to authorize any real use of force, the same
distributed-control logic as multi-key nuclear launch — is only as
strong as the technical trust-chain enforcing it. Any stale, cached,
or long-lived-trust exception anywhere in the Fleet's own
authorization systems (the same category of vulnerability already
built into Lassell Relay's own deliberately-approved exception) would
let a sufficiently positioned insider bypass nine blocs' worth of
political consensus by finding one cached credential instead. Nothing
confirms this has ever actually been exploited — deliberately left
open as the sharpest mechanically-real hook this whole pass produced.

### The Patria Front versus the Good Neighbor Understanding

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. An informal, deliberately
unwritten norm among the four veto powers — none of them will admit
to it on paper, the same way the Sol Industrial Trust's own collusion
stays suspected rather than proven — but the Patria Front doesn't need
a signed document to name what it sees: the Atlantic Zone's own
regional primacy over Patria Grande, dressed in cooperative-sounding
language the same way the real Good Neighbor Policy softened the
Monroe Doctrine's own teeth without changing what it actually meant.
Live and unresolved, aimed at a different target than the Patria
Front's own rivalry with Brazilian dominance, not a restatement of it.

### Venus's unionization fight

Home file: `venus.md`. The platforms being physically tethered
together for mutual buoyancy makes the whole cluster's wealthy
operators face a real collective-action problem: a successful union
win on any one platform's maintenance crew is a credible, visible
precedent that could spread to all of them, so nobody wants to
concede first. The New International is the obvious organizing force
behind the push. Live and unresolved — no concession won yet, not
settled either way.

### The Embassy Platform's known secret

Home file: `venus.md`. A real Cold War Berlin dynamic transplanted
intact: the UN's own DSS listening post hides under a basic UN
embassy, Mars's own MRN Intelligence post hides inside its already-
established research platform, and both sides know exactly what the
other's posting really is without either saying so officially.
Stable precisely because naming it costs more than tolerating it —
nothing confirms that equilibrium has ever actually been tested.

### Verdant Loom versus Albion Weave

Home file: `corporations.md`. A real-market instance of "formal
legitimacy vs. real power," not a boardroom rivalry invented for its
own sake: Verdant Loom's Ganymede-grown technical fabrics are
genuinely better for space-relevant use than anything Albion Weave's
old Atlantic Zone capital produces, and Albion Weave is winning on
market power and old contracts anyway. Live and ongoing, outcome
undecided.

### ODS versus the Jinyiwei on Elysium's own ground

Home file: `mars-political-structure.md`. A concrete, institutional
extension of Elysium's devolution friction with MDR, not a new
invented conflict: Mariner Valley's Office of Doctrinal Security
claims jurisdiction over Elysium alongside Elysium's own Jinyiwei,
two very differently-coded security services (Western-
coalition-flavored versus Sino-Russian-flavored) both operating on the
same ground, neither trusting the other, neither able to fully
dismiss the other's claim. Live and unresolved — nothing confirms
they've ever actually clashed openly.

### The New International's own open/underground split

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. The open, legal wing
increasingly worried the underground's actions hand the UN a ready
pretext for crackdowns; the underground increasingly views the open
wing as a legitimacy-laundering dead end. Live tension inside one
organization for now, not yet a clean split — whether it becomes one
is a real, undecided question.

### The Free Commerce Alliance's hidden funding

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. Reads exactly like a
small-business/consumer-advocacy group opposing the Interstation Tax
Compact's enforcement and the Proportionality Caucus's reapportionment
push — secretly funded by the Sol Industrial Trust. A clean,
discoverable investigation hook: nothing confirms the funding trail
has ever actually been exposed.

### The Directorate of Security Services' own capture

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. DSS is funded by all nine
blocs but its real staffing and leadership lean disproportionately
toward the four veto powers, especially the Atlantic Zone — a new,
concrete grievance for the Proportionality Caucus distinct from
funding and representation, and a live question nobody's forced yet:
whether DSS counterintelligence actually protects the Fleet's
authorization systems from the rogue-command vulnerability, or
whether DSS's own compartmentalized, low-visibility nature makes it
the likeliest vector for exploiting that same vulnerability. Not
resolved either way, on purpose.

### Mars's dependency on UN Spaceguard data

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. MDR's own already-flagged
missing early-warning/debris-tracking capability
(`mars-political-structure.md`) means Mars plausibly leans on the UN's
own Spaceguard Directorate for near-object detection it doesn't
generate independently — a real, standing strategic dependency given
Earth-Mars history, not just an engineering gap. The Seven Day War's
own root cause was a timing/trust failure misread as deliberate
hostility; a scenario where Spaceguard data is withheld, delayed, or
simply wrong at the wrong moment rhymes with that founding trauma
without repeating it outright.

### ASA's compound legal ambiguity

Home file: `space-law-compacts.md`. Not one open question but the same
one, recurring by structural design: ASA holds no seat in the Sol
Tripartite Process, so its compliance status is left deliberately
unresolved across every compact that process produced — Additional
Protocol IV (Outer Space, `earth-political-structure.md`), the
Anti-Piracy Compact's universal-jurisdiction standard, and the
Non-Proliferation Agreement's fissile-material bar all have the exact
same shape of open question hanging on them: not "ASA is guilty" or
"ASA is innocent," but "nobody with standing has ever had to decide,"
which is precisely what keeps ASA reading as genuinely outside the
legal order rather than merely unpopular within it.

### Doris as a Freedom of Transit Compact loophole

Home file: `space-law-compacts.md`. A new, concrete function stacked
on Doris's existing flag-of-convenience role (`corporations.md`,
already load-bearing for the Torture Convention's jurisdictional seam
and the Tax Compact's capture): a ship can reflag through Doris
specifically to claim transit rights its true operator's actual bloc
identity wouldn't get. No confirmed instance yet — a live, unexercised
mechanism rather than a past event, same as the Compact's other four
loopholes.

### Hauptmann & Voskuijlen's quiet grip on propulsion and life support

Home file: `corporations.md`, `frontier-timeline.md`. Older and
bigger than any of H&V's other known tendrils (Doris, Piast's
leverage, the Frontier Opportunity Program's financing, Venus's own
private bank, Nova Court's casino stake): real, quiet equity in both
Korolev Propulsion and Vernadsky Systems, financed into existence on
H&V credit before the first colony ship ever burned for Mars, debt
that never fully cleared because neither propulsion nor life support
ever stopped being essential. No single ship or habitat anywhere in
known space is untouched by it, and nothing confirms anyone outside
H&V itself has ever actually mapped how far this specific tendril
reaches.

### Haven Corrections Group's jurisdictional seam under the Torture Convention

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. The UN Convention Against
Torture is one of the rare cases that actually clears the UN's own
high domestic-binding threshold — genuine, enforceable law across all
nine blocs — but Haven Corrections Group operates as an Earth-Mars
joint venture (`corporations.md`), and Mars isn't party to the
Convention at all. No proof of anything yet, just a real, structural
seam no one's actually tested: an unresolved question of which
partner's compliance regime actually governs a joint facility, sitting
right next to the same kind of flag-of-convenience loophole Doris
already represents.

### The New International versus the UN

Home file: `earth-political-structure.md`. Real organized political
opposition the UN doesn't formally permit at this scale, which forces
the New International to run as two things at once: an open, if
marginalized, political movement, and a genuine clandestine
resistance network when it needs to protect members or move
information the UN would otherwise suppress.

### The Rockline Compact's suspicion of the Sol Industrial Trust

Home file: `corporations.md`. A slow-burning economic fault line
with no proof yet, which is exactly what makes it a flashpoint rather
than settled fact: the Compact has noticed how suspiciously well
Oberth Extraction never gets undercut on Belt mining despite sitting
at the supposed frontier of competition, and is quietly coordinating
in self-defense against a collusion it can't yet demonstrate.

### Lunar resource depletion

Home file: none yet — newly identified here, not yet given a
dedicated writeup. Luna's own helium-3 deposits (`armstrong-city.md`)
are real, but genuinely finite: solar-wind-implanted He-3 in regolith
sits at real parts-per-billion concentrations, and decades of
sustained industrial extraction is exactly the process that works
through the easiest, richest deposits first — the same real pattern
as actual terrestrial mining history. A believable, well-earned
consequence: Luna's easy reserves are plausibly running thin by now,
pushing extraction toward harder, lower-grade regolith at rising
cost. That doesn't just coincide with several threads already built
elsewhere — it helps *explain* them: MDR's own push for Phobos/Deimos
fuel independence (`mars-political-structure.md`, and a plausible
fourth Mars-side pressure point on top of the three above, since a
drying Luna raises the internal stakes over who actually controls
Phobos/Deimos), Frostline Harvesting's cartel-style defensiveness
over its regional supply (`europa.md`), and the real strategic stakes
behind the fight over Europa's deuterium all read differently once
Luna's own well is visibly running dry rather than just being
prudently diversified away from. Worth a dedicated pass of its own;
flagged here first so the connection isn't lost before that happens.
Also now the explicit funding motive behind Ganymede's own long-term,
currently-impractical research into mining helium-3 straight from
Jupiter's atmosphere (`ganymede-condominium.md`) — the one reservoir
in the system big enough to actually end this crisis, decades before
the energy economics might ever make it real. Also now the real,
present-day reason Saturn's own atmospheric skimming operation
(`saturn.md`) draws outside capital and attention well out of
proportion to how remote the place is — a smaller reservoir than
Jupiter's, but the one gas giant where the extraction economics
already work.
