# Asteroid Bases: VPH, Davida, and Sylvia's Reach

Status: locked, 2026-11-24. The next tier of asteroid bases after
Ceres and Thisbe — the real next-largest main-belt bodies, each
getting a basic treatment rather than a full page, per the explicit
"even the top ones can be basic" scope for this batch. Also includes
Shoemaker Outpost (Eros), kept deliberately minor and unhighlighted.

**Naming convention for this batch:** bare names, no descriptor —
Ceres's own precedent, not "Station" or any other suffix. For
Vesta, Pallas, and Hygiea, the plainness reflects something real
about the places (terse, hard-luck, no patience for fancy labels);
for Davida, it's simply that nothing about the place earns
embellishment. Sylvia is the one deliberate exception.

## Vesta, Pallas, Hygiea (VPH): the Belt's oldest, hardest-luck trio

**Shared political and historical character, free to use because
it's a real, generic pattern, not an invented one:** the three
largest bodies after Ceres were naturally the first settled —
biggest draw, earliest development — and first-settled infrastructure
is exactly the infrastructure that's had the longest time to age,
decay, and get passed over for newer investment. That's the same real
pattern behind actual inner-city neighborhoods that were the first
wave of urban development ending up the most economically depressed
decades later. VPH are the Belt's oldest, most barebones, hardest-luck
settlements — the natural home base for **ASA's** hardline sentiment,
not by authorial fiat but because genuinely neglected, aging places
reliably produce exactly that kind of politics.

### Vesta

**LocationChain:** `BELT:VESTA` (Patchwright Conversions already
anchors at `BELT:VESTA:PATCHWRIGHT`)

Real facts: second-largest asteroid, and notably the *brightest* —
occasionally even naked-eye visible from Earth. Genuinely unusual for
its size: differentiated like a small planet (crust, mantle, metallic
core), with evidence of ancient volcanic activity. A massive south-
pole impact basin (Rheasilvia) from an ancient collision is the real
source of an entire asteroid family *and* a known class of meteorites
actually found on Earth — confirmed by NASA's real Dawn mission,
which orbited it 2011–2012. The richest, most mining-friendly body of
the three.

### Pallas

**LocationChain:** `BELT:PALLAS` (Cutwater Yards already anchors at
`BELT:PALLAS:CUTWATER`)

Real facts: third-largest, with a genuinely distinctive quirk — a
highly inclined orbit (~34°) relative to the main belt's plane,
unusually tilted for a body this size. That's a free, physically real
reason Pallas feels more remote than its size suggests: reaching it
costs real extra fuel and trajectory planning compared to in-plane
bodies, no invented justification needed. Carbonaceous, volatile-rich.

### Hygiea

**LocationChain:** `BELT:HYGIEA` (Millrace Shipworks already anchors
at `BELT:HYGIEA:MILLRACE`)

Real facts: fourth-largest, and recent real observation found it
surprisingly, almost dwarf-planet-level spherical for its size — a
genuine surprise finding. Carbonaceous, part of a real collisional
family.

## Davida: genuinely ordinary

**LocationChain:** `BELT:DAVIDA`

Real facts: one of the largest carbonaceous asteroids (~326 km), more
irregular/elongated than the VPH trio, dark low-albedo surface. The
most unremarkable of the five — no dramatic hook, no distinctive
political character, just a solid, workaday mining base. The bare
name fits by default here, not by earned plainness the way it does
for VPH.

## Sylvia's Reach: not quite pirate, shadiest of the lot

**LocationChain:** `BELT:SYLVIA:REACH`

Real facts: ~286 km, and the detail that makes the whole concept
work — Sylvia genuinely has two moons of its own (real IAU-named
Romulus and Remus). A body with two orbiting companions is a
genuinely complex, multi-body environment, physically hard to monitor
from outside and easy for locals who actually know the terrain to
disappear into — not an invented justification, a direct consequence
of the real orbital geometry.

**Character:** the real historical Tortuga (the actual 17th-century
Caribbean buccaneer haven, not a fictional reference) is the right
model — not quite outright pirates, but the shadiest, least-policed
settlement of the batch, where "the Reach" earns its name far better
than it would have for Doris's paperwork-mill purpose: genuinely hard
to reach safely, genuinely easy to vanish into once you're there.

## Shoemaker Outpost (Eros)

**LocationChain:** `BELT:EROS:SHOEMAKER`

Deliberately minor, deliberately not called a "Station" — a quiet
reuse of the Eugene Shoemaker honorific already established for
Shoemaker Geological Survey (`corporations.md`), rather than a new
invention. 433 Eros is a real asteroid (the first one any spacecraft
ever orbited and landed on, NASA's real NEAR Shoemaker mission) and
using it as a location is using real astronomical fact, same bucket
as Ceres or Vesta — but it's also Expanse's single most plot-critical
location, so this stays firmly out of the top-five tier, gets no
further elaboration, and is never meant to be highlighted.
