# Asteroid Bases: VPH, Davida, and Sylvia's Reach

Status: locked, 2026-11-24. The next tier of asteroid bases after
Ceres and Thisbe — the real next-largest main-belt bodies, each
getting a basic treatment rather than a full page, per the explicit
"even the top ones can be basic" scope for this batch. Also includes
Shoemaker Outpost (Eros), kept deliberately minor and unhighlighted.

**Naming convention for this batch:** bare names, no descriptor —
Ceres's own precedent, not "Station" or any other suffix. For
Vesta, Pallas, and Hygiea, the plainness reflects something real
about the places (terse, hard-luck, no patience for fancy labels);
for Davida, it's simply that nothing about the place earns
embellishment. Sylvia is the one deliberate exception.

## Vesta, Pallas, Hygiea (VPH): the Belt's oldest, hardest-luck trio

**Shared political and historical character, free to use because
it's a real, generic pattern, not an invented one:** the three
largest bodies after Ceres were naturally the first settled —
biggest draw, earliest development — and first-settled infrastructure
is exactly the infrastructure that's had the longest time to age,
decay, and get passed over for newer investment. That's the same real
pattern behind actual inner-city neighborhoods that were the first
wave of urban development ending up the most economically depressed
decades later. VPH are the Belt's oldest, most barebones, hardest-luck
settlements — the natural home base for **ASA's** hardline sentiment,
not by authorial fiat but because genuinely neglected, aging places
reliably produce exactly that kind of politics.

### Vesta

**LocationChain:** `BELT:VESTA` (Patchwright Conversions already
anchors at `BELT:VESTA:PATCHWRIGHT`)

Real facts: second-largest asteroid, and notably the *brightest* —
occasionally even naked-eye visible from Earth. Genuinely unusual for
its size: differentiated like a small planet (crust, mantle, metallic
core), with evidence of ancient volcanic activity. A massive south-
pole impact basin (Rheasilvia) from an ancient collision is the real
source of an entire asteroid family *and* a known class of meteorites
actually found on Earth — confirmed by NASA's real Dawn mission,
which orbited it 2011–2012. The richest, most mining-friendly body of
the three — differentiation means an actual metallic core, real ore,
not just carbonaceous rubble.

**History:** that richness is exactly why Vesta was the first of the
three settled, and drew the biggest early investment by a wide
margin — a genuine early-Belt-expansion-era charter operation,
generations before the modern Trust-consolidated corporate landscape
(`corporations.md`) existed. Vesta was worth spinning up properly:
a full, genuine body-scale rotational habitat, a real engineering
commitment matching the scale of the initial bet (Cole Spindle Works'
predecessor-era work, if it predates the firm itself — still open).
The ore and refining economy that followed made Vesta a real
industrial anchor, not just a mining camp, which is exactly why
Patchwright's modern ship-conversion yard makes sense sited here:
metal-rich, industrially mature ground.

**The decline, and why it hit hardest here.** Once richer or easier
finds opened up elsewhere — Thisbe's helium-3 (`thisbe-station.md`),
Psyche's metals under Oberth Extraction (`corporations.md`) — capital
followed the better return. Vesta's ore didn't stop being valuable;
it just stopped being the *best* available use of fresh investment,
so the ore kept getting shipped out while the aging first-wave
infrastructure that extracted it got passed over for reinvestment —
the sharpest, angriest version of VPH's shared pattern, because
Vesta is also the richest of the three: producing the most and
visibly getting the least reinvested back is a harder thing to
swallow than Pallas's or Hygiea's quieter neglect.

**Who actually runs it today:** on paper, a skeleton administrative
presence representing a distant Trust-affiliated portfolio holder —
the original charter long since absorbed as a legacy asset
(`corporations.md`'s hidden-ownership logic). In practice, day-to-day
legitimacy on the ground has drifted to **ASA-aligned worker
councils**, the same formal-ownership-vs-real-local-authority split
already in play for MDR and Elysium (`mars-political-structure.md`) —
just surfacing here as class resentment instead of planetary
politics. Vesta is ASA's angriest, most organized asteroid cell, not
its most isolated one.

### Pallas

**LocationChain:** `BELT:PALLAS` (Cutwater Yards already anchors at
`BELT:PALLAS:CUTWATER`)

Real facts: third-largest, with a genuinely distinctive quirk — a
highly inclined orbit (~34°) relative to the main belt's plane,
unusually tilted for a body this size. That's a free, physically real
reason Pallas feels more remote than its size suggests: reaching it
costs real extra fuel and trajectory planning compared to in-plane
bodies, no invented justification needed. Carbonaceous, volatile-rich.

**History:** settled in roughly the same early wave as Vesta, but
that same orbital inclination made it the stepchild from day one —
nobody committed flagship-scale capital to a body this costly to
reach. No full-body spin-up; what habitat Pallas has is tunneled-in,
improvised internal centrifuge sections added piecemeal as needed,
never the single expensive engineering commitment Vesta got.
Ownership never consolidated into one clean charter either — a
patchwork of smaller claims and loose cooperative-style arrangements
from the start, a scrappier, less-organized precursor to what
Thisbe's cooperative later became properly. What passive-giant money
eventually bought in picked up fragments piecemeal, not the whole
rock.

**Production:** volatile and water extraction, consumed mostly as a
deliberate resupply stop rather than a through-route — ships detour
to Pallas on purpose for fuel and life-support consumables, since its
tilt means nobody passes it by accident the way belt traffic passes
through an in-plane body.

**Character and governance:** less "wealth extracted, nothing
reinvested" anger than Vesta, more defiant self-reliant pride — the
flavor of hardline sentiment that comes from a place that never
expected help and resents being reminded it didn't get any. With the
least corporate presence of the three to begin with, Pallas is
arguably the closest thing in the Belt to genuine ASA self-governance
in practice, not just in sympathy — there's less a councils-vs-owners
split here than at Vesta, because there's barely an owner left to
split from.

### Hygiea

**LocationChain:** `BELT:HYGIEA` (Millrace Shipworks already anchors
at `BELT:HYGIEA:MILLRACE`)

Real facts: fourth-largest, and recent real observation found it
surprisingly, almost dwarf-planet-level spherical for its size — a
genuine surprise finding. Carbonaceous, part of a real collisional
family.

**History:** settled contemporaneously with Vesta and Pallas but
drew only modest investment — enough for a basic, utilitarian spin-up
(good enough for industrial function, no flagship ambition behind
it), no boom, no dramatic bust. One of the passive giants
(`corporations.md`'s Bedrock or Longview) picked it up early as a
bulk-commodity asset and has run it lean ever since: minimal staff,
minimal attention, a quiet line item rather than a strategic holding.

**Production:** bulk water and volatile extraction off its
carbonaceous, collisional-family composition — low-margin, high-
volume, the Belt's quiet plumbing rather than anything glamorous.
Millrace's shipworks siting fits the same workaday character.

**Character and governance:** the quietest of the three politically,
but just as real — not anger at broken promises, not pride in self-
reliance, just the grinding sense of being genuinely overlooked. With
almost no corporate attention on the ground, ASA cells function as
the only organized local authority largely by default, which makes
Hygiea's rank-and-file some of ASA's most reliable, if least vocal.

## Davida: genuinely ordinary

**LocationChain:** `BELT:DAVIDA`

Real facts: one of the largest carbonaceous asteroids (~326 km), more
irregular/elongated than the VPH trio, dark low-albedo surface. The
most unremarkable of the five — no dramatic hook, no distinctive
political character, just a solid, workaday mining base. The bare
name fits by default here, not by earned plainness the way it does
for VPH.

## Sylvia's Reach: not quite pirate, shadiest of the lot

**LocationChain:** `BELT:SYLVIA:REACH`

Real facts: ~286 km, and the detail that makes the whole concept
work — Sylvia genuinely has two moons of its own (real IAU-named
Romulus and Remus). A body with two orbiting companions is a
genuinely complex, multi-body environment, physically hard to monitor
from outside and easy for locals who actually know the terrain to
disappear into — not an invented justification, a direct consequence
of the real orbital geometry.

**Character:** the real historical Tortuga (the actual 17th-century
Caribbean buccaneer haven, not a fictional reference) is the right
model — not quite outright pirates, but the shadiest, least-policed
settlement of the batch, where "the Reach" earns its name far better
than it would have for Doris's paperwork-mill purpose: genuinely hard
to reach safely, genuinely easy to vanish into once you're there.

## Shoemaker Outpost (Eros)

**LocationChain:** `BELT:EROS:SHOEMAKER`

Deliberately minor, deliberately not called a "Station" — a quiet
reuse of the Eugene Shoemaker honorific already established for
Shoemaker Geological Survey (`corporations.md`), rather than a new
invention. 433 Eros is a real asteroid (the first one any spacecraft
ever orbited and landed on, NASA's real NEAR Shoemaker mission) and
using it as a location is using real astronomical fact, same bucket
as Ceres or Vesta — but it's also Expanse's single most plot-critical
location, so this stays firmly out of the top-five tier, gets no
further elaboration, and is never meant to be highlighted.

## Outer system: future pass (flagged, not yet built)

A further batch of real small bodies, noted 2026-11-24 while looking
at the map, each real and free to use — flagged as a future outer-
system pass rather than built out now:

- **944 Hidalgo** — a real transitional asteroid/Centaur-adjacent
  object: extremely eccentric, highly inclined (~42°) orbit that
  swings from near Jupiter out toward Saturn's distance. Nothing on
  an orbit like that can be serviced on a normal schedule.
- **2101 Adonis** — a real, small (~0.6 km) near-Earth asteroid, one
  of the first NEAs ever discovered (1936), crossing both Earth's and
  Venus's orbits.
- **1221 Amor** — namesake of the real "Amor group": NEAs whose
  orbits approach Earth's but stay just outside it at closest
  approach.
- **The Jupiter Trojans and Greeks** — real asteroid swarms trapped
  at Jupiter's L4 ("Greek camp," leading) and L5 ("Trojan camp,"
  trailing) Lagrange points, stable for billions of years, named for
  Trojan War figures — including the real historical naming quirk of
  two permanent "spies," 617 Patroclus (a Greek name, sitting in the
  Trojan camp) and 624 Hektor (a Trojan name, sitting in the Greek
  camp). Same stable-Lagrange-point siting logic already used for
  Vishvakarma at Earth-Moon L4, scaled up to Jupiter's distance.
- **The Hilda group / "Hilda's Triangle"** — a real asteroid group in
  3:2 resonance with Jupiter, sitting between the main belt and the
  Trojans; plotted in Jupiter's rotating frame, the group traces an
  actual triangle rather than a ring — resonance-*trapped* rather
  than resonance-*cleared*, the inverse of the Kirkwood-gap logic
  behind Slayton.

**Working vision for the pass:** small, independent mining operations
working these regions, each cluster anchored by a minor "traveler's
inn"-type waypoint station sited at the natural entry point into that
region — not full bases, just the Belt's version of a highway rest
stop before the next long haul. The Hilda group in particular sits
squarely on the natural path out to the Trojans, which would finally
give the still-undeveloped relay-town archetype (RFC-2353's hop-count
mechanics, flagged in the lore README) a physical home.
